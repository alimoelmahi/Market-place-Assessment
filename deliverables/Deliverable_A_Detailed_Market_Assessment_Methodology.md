# Detailed Market Assessment Methodology for Khartoum Markets

## Methodological Basis

The assessment will use a structured secondary-evidence approach to examine selected markets in Khartoum State and their relationships with surrounding populations, suppliers, transport networks, services, and livelihood systems. It will combine documentary analysis, existing administrative and humanitarian datasets, geospatial modelling, interpretation of existing satellite imagery, and comparative market-system analysis. No new interviews, questionnaires, focus groups, site inspections, or field photography will be undertaken. Telephone interviews and online consultations intended to generate new assessment observations would constitute primary research and are excluded from this methodology.

The approach is designed to support recovery planning, rehabilitation pre-feasibility, and transparent investment screening under access constraints. It draws on the supplied UNDP market assessment framework and concept note, and on relevant humanitarian market-assessment principles, including the dimensions described in WFP's Market Functionality Index. It does not constitute a corporate WFP MFI exercise, certification of compliance with a particular assessment instrument, or formal UNDP methodological approval.

The methodology will distinguish the condition and needs of a market from the readiness of a particular rehabilitation intervention. It will also distinguish directly documented findings, remotely observed physical features, modelled estimates, analytical assumptions, and unresolved evidence gaps. This distinction will apply throughout analysis, interpretation, and reporting.

The methodology is prospective. Its specification does not imply that datasets have been acquired, analyses completed, market conditions verified, or investment decisions approved. The initial reference-review cutoff is 27 October 2026; the dates of the underlying observations will be recorded separately and may be earlier.

## 1. Assessment Objectives

### 1.1 Overall Objective

To establish a transparent, spatially explicit, and evidence-qualified understanding of the condition, functionality, accessibility, economic role, and recovery potential of selected Khartoum State markets, and to identify conditional rehabilitation and livelihood-recovery options using secondary information exclusively.

### 1.2 Specific Objectives

| Objective | Assessment purpose | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Establish the market baseline | Identify market locations, boundaries, roles, administrative affiliations, and documented pre-conflict conditions. | Define a defensible assessment universe and the appropriate unit for analysis. |
| Assess functionality and physical conditions | Examine documented operation, essential commodity availability, visible infrastructure damage, and supporting-service continuity. | Distinguish repair needs, service constraints, and operating limitations. |
| Examine accessibility and potential demand | Estimate physical access and potential resident catchments under explicit population, road, and travel assumptions. | Identify access gaps and compare potential reach without claiming actual customer numbers. |
| Understand market-system connections | Identify documented suppliers, commodity origins, intermediate hubs, transport dependencies, and feasible modelled alternatives. | Locate bottlenecks and assess whether site rehabilitation would address a wider system constraint. |
| Assess economic and livelihood conditions | Review existing evidence on businesses, employment, trader capacity, finance, digital services, and barriers affecting different groups. | Develop market-linked livelihood options and identify questions that cannot be answered remotely. |
| Assess vulnerability and disruption | Examine recorded conflict exposure, flood and climate pressures, service interruptions, environmental constraints, and documented damage. | Identify risks, mitigation requirements, and conditions that may prevent implementation. |
| Examine recovery and resilience | Compare dated observations and scenario performance to assess continuity, redundancy, and plausible recovery pathways. | Identify interventions that retain value under uncertain recovery conditions. |
| Support transparent prioritization | Compare conditional opportunities using declared criteria while keeping humanitarian need, evidence confidence, and investment safeguards separate. | Prepare a justified shortlist or an evidence-resolution/enabling-work portfolio. |

### 1.3 Intended Use and Decision Boundaries

The assessment will support strategic planning, program design, donor dialogue, preparation of market-specific intervention concepts, and identification of further due-diligence requirements. It may identify where rehabilitation appears justified, where service or livelihood interventions may be more appropriate than construction, and where evidence is insufficient to recommend a preferred option.

It will not independently establish trader-level eligibility, representative household preferences, current business turnover, structural safety, explosive-ordnance clearance, lawful undisputed tenure, or safe movement. Nor will it establish the causal effect of rehabilitation on employment, income, prices, or return. Any statutory or technical requirement for subsequent inspection, clearance, consent, or specialist approval will remain a condition for implementation.

## 2. Key Research Questions

Research questions will be answered at the most specific geographic and temporal scale supported by available evidence. A question will remain unanswered where the evidence is absent or unsuitable; a plausible proxy will not automatically be treated as an equivalent measurement.

### 2.1 Assessment Matrix

| Research area | Key research questions | Evidence required to answer | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market identity and hierarchy | Which distinct markets are included? Where are their actual trading areas and entrances? What retail, wholesale, aggregation, distribution, or specialized roles are documented? | Consistent names and aliases; existing maps and records; interpretable imagery; role descriptions with dates. | A neighborhood name or map pin alone may not identify a market boundary or entrance. |
| Operating status | Is each market documented as open, partially operating, closed, or of unknown current status? Which observation dates support that classification? | Market-specific existing monitoring, administrative records, or assessment reports. | Visible structures and nearby illumination are not proof of trading. |
| Physical condition | Which market assets show visible damage or loss? What part of the market can be interpreted reliably? Which essential facilities are documented as deficient? | Suitable pre/post imagery or existing damage products; baseline inventory; existing technical or service records. | Observable roof condition does not establish structural integrity or internal service condition. |
| Commodity supply | Which goods are explicitly documented as available? What essential or nutritious groups appear constrained? How complete is the monitoring basket? | Existing availability observations matched to market, product, period, and market type. | Missing observations are not stockouts, and a reported price is not proof of adequate stock. |
| Prices and purchasing power | How have comparable prices changed? Is instability evident? What can be established about affordability using applicable wage, income, or transfer evidence? | Comparable item-price panels and, for affordability, a defensible purchasing-power denominator. | Nominal price change is not necessarily a change in real purchasing power. |
| Consumer access | Which settlements or resident populations can physically reach a market under the model? Where do rivers, disconnected roads, and bridge dependencies constrain access? | Verified entrances; reviewed transport topology; population surfaces/scenarios; mode-specific travel assumptions. | Modelled reach is neither safe access nor demonstrated consumer use. |
| Supplier connectivity | Which production areas, import gateways, intermediate hubs, and routes are documented? Where are supply bottlenecks and single points of failure? | Dated commodity-flow reports and transport/access information; a separate modelled road graph. | A shortest path is a potential route, not a measured commodity flow. |
| Business ecosystem | What is documented about trading activity, supporting businesses, logistics, financial services, and working-capital constraints? | Existing enterprise/market records; confirmed service status; dated business and facility inventories. | Mapped business density cannot quantify sales, profits, capital, or competition. |
| Inclusion and protection | What existing evidence identifies barriers for women, youth, persons with disabilities, informal vendors, displaced people, returnees, and host communities? | Applicable disaggregated studies and records; documented protection/access constraints. | Demographic composition cannot establish trader participation or equal access. |
| Conflict disruption | What qualifying incidents and documented interruptions affect market neighborhoods and supply corridors? How do precision and reporting gaps affect the interpretation? | Reviewed conflict-event records and corroborating humanitarian reports. | No recorded event does not establish absence of violence, coercion, or explosive hazards. |
| Environmental conditions | Which markets, access routes, and potential catchments are exposed to modelled river flooding, observed flooding, heat, water constraints, or waste-management pressures? | Hazard products, observed-event extents, climate/surface observations, and existing utility/environmental records. | Exposure screening is not a site-specific hazard, drainage, water-quality, or engineering assessment. |
| Recovery and resilience | Do repeated observations support a recovery trajectory? Which services and routes offer alternatives? Which conditions could reverse apparent improvement? | Time-series observations; documented service restoration; route and population scenarios. | One image or one reopening report cannot establish sustained recovery. |
| Rehabilitation and livelihoods | What problem would a proposed intervention solve? Is construction necessary? What complementary finance, services, access, or livelihood measures are indicated? | Evidenced deficit; plausible demand/system role; existing feasibility and institutional records; alternatives comparison. | An attractive concept is not a validated business case or costed engineering design. |
| Prioritization | Which markets have the strongest evidenced rehabilitation case, and which require enabling conditions or further evidence? How stable is the comparison under alternative assumptions? | Eligible indicators, declared weights, uncertainty analysis, and independent investment gates. | A high readiness score does not necessarily mean greatest humanitarian need or entitlement to funding. |

### 2.2 Cross-Cutting Questions

For every thematic finding, the assessment will ask:

1. What is observed or documented, rather than inferred?
2. Which market, population, geographic area, and observation period does the evidence actually describe?
3. Could the apparent pattern result from reporting intensity, data vintage, seasonal conditions, sensor differences, or mapping completeness?
4. Which groups or activities may be absent from the source, particularly informal traders and people facing mobility or digital-access barriers?
5. What evidence would contradict the interpretation, and has that alternative explanation been examined?
6. Which decision is supported, and which decision remains conditional?

## 3. Analytical Framework

### 3.1 Assessment Design

The assessment will be a non-experimental, multi-source, comparative study with spatial and temporal components. Quantitative analysis will be used where variables have valid units, denominators, and geographic support. Qualitative documentary analysis will explain institutional arrangements, constraints, inclusion issues, and intervention dependencies. Remote-sensing interpretation will address observable physical conditions and contextual change; network and population models will address conditional accessibility and potential catchments.

The design will not use the appearance of numerical precision to overcome weak evidence. Numerical outputs will be withheld, bounded, or explicitly labelled historical when their input conditions are not met.

### 3.2 Market-System Perspective

Each market will be examined as a node within a wider system comprising:

- The core exchange function: producers, aggregators, wholesalers, retailers, service businesses, and consumers.
- Supporting functions: transport, loading, storage, energy, water, sanitation, waste management, finance, digital connectivity, information, and business services.
- Formal and informal rules: land rights, permissions, market management, fees, trading-space allocation, entry barriers, and documented social or institutional constraints.
- The external operating environment: conflict, population movement, macroeconomic pressure, purchasing power, seasonality, flood and climate exposure, and humanitarian assistance where relevant.

The analysis will test whether an identified limitation is principally a physical-asset deficit, a service failure, a supply or demand constraint, an institutional barrier, an access problem, or a combination. This diagnosis will precede intervention selection. For example, adding covered trading space will not be assumed to increase use when existing documentation indicates that vendors prefer a more accessible location.

### 3.3 Levels and Units of Analysis

| Level | Analytical unit | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| Market site | A verified market identity and existing trading footprint | Operating status, physical condition, service evidence, and rehabilitation needs. |
| Market sub-site | A separately identified section, entrance, loading area, or proposed parcel linked to one market | Examine differing conditions without inflating the number of markets. |
| Market neighborhood | A declared buffer or functionally relevant surrounding area | Local access burden, supporting services, and appropriately scaled activity proxies. |
| Consumer catchment | Origins within a modelled travel-time threshold or unique nearest-market allocation | Potential resident reach and access gaps. |
| Supply network | Documented origins, intermediate hubs, destination markets, and modelled route alternatives | Corridor dependencies and system-wide constraints. |
| Locality/state | Adopted administrative geography with stable identifiers | Contextual reporting, population reconciliation, and coverage assessment. |
| Observation period | Market-indicator-period record, linked to a source and scenario where relevant | Preserve temporal validity and support comparison. |

The market register will retain original Arabic names, English working names, aliases, source identifiers, candidate locations, geometry status, and final administrative codes. The supplied concept note refers to 15 markets but contains 14 identifiable entries. The final cohort will therefore be confirmed before location-dependent analysis. An unresolved fifteenth site will not be created to match the intended sample size.

The selected markets constitute a purposive cohort, not a statistical sample of every market in Khartoum State. State-wide coverage claims will require evidence that the final inventory actually captures the relevant universe. The market universe and any exclusions will be frozen for each analytical release.

### 3.4 Temporal Comparison

Four temporal layers will be maintained: a pre-conflict baseline, dated disruption observations, the latest eligible observation period, and prospective scenarios. The pre-conflict baseline will use comparable observations from 2022 and/or January-March 2023, where available, without including post-15-April-2023 conditions as a pre-conflict reference.

Comparison windows will be matched by season and, where practical, calendar month. Harvest cycles, rainy-season effects, holidays, and changing observation frequency will be considered before interpreting change as recovery or deterioration. The date of satellite acquisition will not be replaced by the date of image download, and the publication date of a report will not be treated as its observation date.

The latest evidence will remain source-specific. A figure containing an older building inventory, a recent population scenario, and a flood climatology will state those different temporal supports rather than imply that all components describe one contemporaneous survey.

### 3.5 Evidence and Inference Classes

| Code | Evidence class | Reporting convention |
|---|---|---|
| D | Documented observation or claim from an existing source | State the original observation period and source; distinguish the source's finding from the assessment's interpretation. |
| R | Remotely observed physical feature or change | Identify imagery/product, date, spatial support, observable coverage, and interpretation uncertainty. |
| M | Modelled estimate or derived proxy | State inputs, formula, scenario, units, and the phenomenon the result does and does not represent. |
| A | Analytical assumption | Identify the rationale, approval status, alternatives, and sensitivity test. |
| U | Unknown or unsupported | Explain the missing evidence and the consequence for interpretation or decision-making. |

Each atomic observation will have one primary evidence class. A composite result may depend on several classes, but will retain the provenance and class of each input rather than obscure them under a single unqualified label. Evidence completeness and evidence quality will be reported separately from substantive market performance.

### 3.6 Results Logic

The assessment will examine a conditional pathway from market constraints to intervention options and potential recovery contributions:

**Evidenced constraint -> feasible response option -> restored asset/service or improved connection -> potential trading benefit -> livelihood contribution requiring subsequent verification.**

The first four links can be appraised using suitable secondary evidence and explicit models. Actual increases in income, employment, business survival, food access, or equitable participation will not be asserted as achieved outcomes without applicable existing outcome records. Baseline-to-current change will not, by itself, establish attribution to an intervention.

### 3.7 Need, Opportunity, and Investment Readiness

Three analytical judgments will remain distinct:

1. **Recovery need:** the severity of documented deficits, exclusion, disruption, and livelihood constraints.
2. **Conditional opportunity/readiness:** the potential benefit and practicality of rehabilitation under specified assumptions.
3. **Investment decision:** a programmatic judgment incorporating evidence, safeguards, additionality, budget, alternatives, equity, and approvals.

The agreed six-criterion screening structure will retain the requested weights: potential population reach 20%, accessibility 15%, adapted functionality 20%, lower recorded conflict exposure 15%, economic significance 15%, and rehabilitation feasibility 15%.

$$S_m=0.20P_m+0.15A_m+0.20F_m+0.15C_m+0.15E_m+0.15R_m.$$

Scores will be oriented so that a higher value indicates stronger conditional opportunity/readiness. In particular, the conflict component will be inversely directed: greater recorded exposure will not improve readiness. The specific normalization anchors, component rubrics, and eligibility rules will be approved and version-controlled before ranking. No score will authorize works or override an unresolved safeguard. High-need markets with low readiness will remain visible in an enabling-conditions portfolio.

## 4. Market Assessment Dimensions

### 4.1 Market Functionality

Functionality will be assessed as the ability of a market to support exchange of relevant goods and services during a defined period. It will be examined through separate evidence on operation, commodity availability, price behavior, service continuity, supply constraints, and the conditions affecting access.

Operating status will use the categories documented open, documented partial, documented closed, historical only, and unknown. Partial operation may cover reduced activity, a functioning section within a larger disrupted complex, or restricted trading, but the original meaning will be retained. Reports using different definitions will not be mechanically converted into a common percentage of operation.

Where a composite functionality proxy is used for screening, it will be explicitly described as an adaptation based on eligible evidence. It will not be reported as the original questionnaire score or the WFP corporate MFI. The component findings will remain available so that a composite does not conceal critical service, food-quality, or protection gaps.

### 4.2 Physical Infrastructure and Supporting Services

The assessment will distinguish trading shelters and buildings from roads, loading areas, storage, drainage, water, sanitation, electricity, and waste services. Existing and proposed assets will be recorded separately. Physical presence, visible condition, documented operation, and adequacy for the proposed use will be treated as different attributes.

Visible condition will be assessed only where image resolution, registration, dates, and viewing conditions support the interpretation. Baseline structures will be classified as observable or unobservable before any damage fraction is calculated. Apparent intactness will mean only that qualifying visible damage was not identified in the observable feature; it will not establish internal condition, structural safety, or fitness for occupation.

Where adequate imagery or applicable existing damage products cannot be obtained, the assessment will report neighborhood-scale disturbance and documentary infrastructure gaps instead of inventing structure-level damage estimates. Cost estimates will be used only when their original scope, quantities, price date, and preparation method are known.

### 4.3 Accessibility, Transport, and Connectivity

Accessibility analysis will model physical movement from population origins to verified market entrances, and separately from supplier origins toward destination markets. Walking and freight modes will have distinct restrictions, speeds, and scenarios. River crossings, bridge status, one-way rules, barriers, and disconnected network segments will be reviewed explicitly.

Service areas will be developed for 15-, 30-, and 60-minute walking thresholds as planning scenarios, not observed travel preferences. Slower walking and road/bridge disruption scenarios will test sensitivity. Unknown passability will remain an uncertainty rather than automatically become an open road.

An all-market physical network may be used for infrastructure planning. A separate conservative service scenario will include only markets with eligible documented operation. Access to an unknown-status market will not be presented as confirmed access to a functioning service. Transport affordability and protection constraints will be analysed from existing evidence, not assumed resolved by network connectivity.

### 4.4 Population Distribution and Potential Consumer Catchments

Potential demand will be framed through the location and estimated presence of residents, not inferred transactions. One population baseline will be selected for each model, with alternative products used for sensitivity rather than added together. Population projections, displacement estimates, and return figures will first be reconciled by geography, period, residency definition, and whether they represent stocks or flows.

Where valid contemporaneous resident totals exist, baseline distribution may be reallocated to low, central, and high population scenarios while preserving each scenario's administrative totals. Where they do not exist, the analysis will remain a dated baseline or a hypothetical population-change scenario, not an estimate of current occupancy.

The assessment will report both individual overlapping catchments and unique nearest-market allocations, with clear labels. Aggregate potential residents will be calculated using a spatial union or unique allocation, never by summing overlapping catchment totals. Results will be described as potential reachable residents; they will not be labelled actual customers, daily footfall, or unique program beneficiaries.

### 4.5 Commodity Availability, Diversity, Prices, and Affordability

Commodity analysis will use a predefined basket appropriate to each market type. General food markets may be examined across staples, pulses, oils, and relevant fresh and nutritious food groups. Livestock and fish markets will use role-specific groups or products. Assortment will not be compared through raw commodity counts across unlike markets.

Availability will require explicit observations of presence or absence within a stated period. Price observations will be standardized by item, grade, retail/wholesale level, unit, market, currency, and period before comparison. Missing data will not be coded as unavailable goods. Official and parallel exchange-rate series will be kept separate where used.

Price trends will distinguish nominal changes from real changes. Volatility calculations will require a sufficiently complete comparable series. Affordability will be examined only where a relevant income, wage, or transfer denominator exists for a compatible population and period. Without such a denominator, the assessment will report price or basket-cost pressure and state that purchasing power is unresolved.

### 4.6 Supply Chains and Trade Routes

Supply-chain analysis will identify documented commodity origins, aggregators, intermediate hubs, destination markets, storage/logistics services, and transport links. Each connection will carry the commodity, season, observation period, source, reported route/status, and quantity only where actually recorded.

Reported links will be differentiated from modelled route alternatives. A historic trade map will support a historical-system description unless contemporary evidence establishes continued relevance. Quantitative flow diagrams will require compatible volumes; otherwise connections will be presented schematically without invented tonnage or relative flow widths.

The analysis will consider whether a market depends on one bridge, corridor, supplier area, service provider, or logistics hub. Route redundancy will not be equated with supplier diversification: two roads can still lead to one source, and several sources can depend on one vulnerable crossing.

### 4.7 Economic Activity, Business Ecosystem, and Competition

Economic activity will be examined through documented operation, existing enterprise records, commercial/logistics mapped presence, service availability, and suitably scaled activity proxies. Mapped businesses will be de-duplicated and separated from confirmed operating businesses. Informal activity, which is frequently underrepresented in mapping, will be acknowledged explicitly.

Nighttime lights will be used only as a contextual neighborhood-scale signal when product quality and spatial support permit it. Changes may reflect electricity restoration, surrounding residential lighting, generators, fires, or sensor differences. They will not be converted into market turnover, GDP, employment, or trader numbers.

Competition will be assessed from existing evidence on ownership, market shares, entry barriers, fees, or trading-space allocation. The number of buildings, mapped shops, or commodity groups will not be used to calculate concentration or infer the absence of cartels. Where applicable firm-level data do not exist, competition and market power will remain qualitative or unknown.

### 4.8 Financial Services and Digital Markets

The assessment will identify existing evidence on banks, agents, microfinance, savings/cooperative arrangements, payment services, and business-finance products relevant to the market area. A listed or mapped branch will be recorded as institutional presence; it will be coded as operating only when appropriate dated evidence supports that status.

Documented product terms, access requirements, liquidity constraints, and reported barriers will be distinguished from actual uptake. Mobile coverage will be treated as potential network availability, not proof of reliable connectivity, digital-payment adoption, affordability, or equal access. Grant, lending, guarantee, or digital-finance options will remain conditional on a demonstrated problem and provider capacity rather than being prescribed uniformly.

### 4.9 Livelihoods, Inclusion, and Protection

Livelihood analysis will link opportunities to documented demand or supply-chain constraints. It will consider activities such as trade, aggregation, handling, repair, logistics, specialized market services, and safe rehabilitation work where appropriate. Opportunities will be screened for working-capital needs, utility dependencies, operating costs, labor safeguards, and the risks of displacing existing livelihoods.

Existing disaggregated evidence will be used to examine barriers affecting women, youth, persons with disabilities, informal vendors, displaced people, returnees, and host communities. Age definitions and denominators will be preserved; the original survey's youth threshold will not be assumed equivalent to every external dataset's definition. No share of women or youth traders will be derived from population age/sex structure.

Protection analysis will consider documented constraints, unequal trading-space access, harassment or exclusion, affordability, mobility, and land-related risks where evidence exists. Accessible physical design will not be treated as proof of social access or safety. Recommendations will include equitable participation safeguards without claiming that the preferences or consent of affected groups have been established by this desk study.

### 4.10 Conflict Exposure and Market Disruption

Conflict analysis will examine appropriately defined violent events and documentary evidence of closures, looting, restrictions, transport interruptions, and damage. Event location and date precision will determine the scale at which an event can be used. Market-neighborhood exposure and supply-corridor disruption will be analysed separately.

Recent 90- and 180-day windows and a 365-day contextual view will be used to examine temporal patterns, subject to source coverage and reporting lag. Buffer distances will be analytical choices tested through sensitivity, not interpreted as safety zones. Broadly geocoded events will not be treated as precisely located near a market simply because a dataset displays a point.

No absence-of-record finding will certify security. Sensitive operational details and vulnerable-person information will not be published. Security/access and explosive-ordnance requirements will remain independent implementation conditions requiring appropriately qualified and current evidence.

### 4.11 Flood, Climate, Water, and Environmental Conditions

The assessment will distinguish hazard, exposed assets/populations, vulnerability, and confirmed impacts. Modelled river-flood scenarios will be analysed separately from observed flood-event extents. Global river-flood models will not be assumed to capture local stormwater accumulation, blocked drainage, altered embankments, or all urban flood processes.

Rainfall will be examined at its actual spatial support, including upstream basin context where relevant to Nile-related flooding. Land-cover and vegetation observations may inform producer-area and environmental context but will not establish market income or food-security outcomes. Surface temperature will indicate hot surfaces under observed conditions, not measured occupational heat stress.

Water analysis will distinguish surface-water occurrence from potable-water availability and documented utility operation. Waste, wastewater, hazardous debris, drainage, fire access, fuel handling, and sustainable water/energy requirements will inform rehabilitation options. Clearing rubble will not be recommended as community work where hazardous materials or explosive remnants have not been addressed through qualified processes.

### 4.12 Resilience, Recovery Potential, and Rehabilitation Feasibility

Resilience will be examined through documented continuity, repeated status observations, route alternatives, service redundancy, and evidence of diversified supply. Recovery will require a sequence of observations. Where observations are intermittent, recovery timing will be bracketed between the last disrupted and first recovered observation rather than assigned an exact day.

Feasibility will examine existing records concerning land/permission, engineering preparation, operations and maintenance, environmental/social mitigation, and the additionality of a proposed intervention. Physical damage severity will not automatically imply a feasible or cost-effective construction project. Apparently intact infrastructure will not rule out a documented need for improved services or site arrangements.

Options will be compared against no-build or service-based alternatives and assessed under relevant adverse scenarios. Where a high-need market has unresolved conditions, the recommendation will identify the enabling action required rather than either exclude the population from consideration or label the site investment-ready.

## 5. Secondary Data Collection Methodology

### 5.1 Search Protocol and Evidence Coverage

A documented search protocol will define the study geography, confirmed market names and aliases, time windows, thematic questions, eligible languages, repositories, and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Searches will cover Arabic and English material and distinguish pre-conflict baseline sources from post-April-2023 observations.

Search strings will combine geographic and market-name variants with terms related to operation, reopening, prices, availability, infrastructure, utilities, supply, transport, livelihoods, displacement, returns, conflict, flooding, and rehabilitation. Every search will record the repository, query, date, period covered, and relevant results. Searches that yield no usable information will also be recorded to avoid presenting a data gap as evidence of absent activity.

The evidence search will have two tracks: a market-specific track for observations that can support individual profiles, and a contextual track for locality, state, national, or upstream-system information. Contextual sources will not be assigned to individual markets as if they were site observations.

### 5.2 Principal Source Families

| Source family | Intended use | Required collection checks |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied UNDP framework, Arabic concept note, and reference StoryMap | Objectives, questionnaire crosswalk, intended cohort, and attributed prior findings. | Distinguish plans from completed observations; confirm observation dates and reuse permissions; retain name/locality ambiguities. |
| OCHA/HDX and available government publications | Administrative boundaries, PCodes, population statistics, facilities, and existing market records. | Record exact resource/version, original publisher, geography, date, license, and whether the file was actually obtained. |
| WorldPop and eligible alternative settlement/population products | Population baseline and distribution sensitivity. | Check counts versus density, reference year, constrained/unconstrained model, geographic coverage, and implications of conflict movement. |
| IOM DTM and relevant UN population information | Movement context and compatible components of population scenarios. | Distinguish current resident stocks from movements/cumulative returns; reconcile dates, definitions, and administrative units. |
| WFP, FEWS NET, FAO, IFPRI, and applicable market-monitoring reports | Commodity availability, prices, market-system context, purchasing-power evidence, and supply links. | Verify named-market coverage, item definitions, units, collection method, period, and whether availability is actually measured. |
| OpenStreetMap and existing transport/logistics publications | Road topology, mapped buildings/facilities, reported links, and access constraints. | Audit completeness, access tags, bridge/tunnel separation, chronology, operating status, and database/license obligations. |
| UNOSAT and authorized archival imagery | Existing damage/flood products and observable physical conditions. | Check analysis and acquisition dates, resolution, interpretation categories, uncertainty, license, and permission to publish derivatives. |
| Copernicus, USGS, NASA, and relevant land/climate products | Environmental context, disturbance screening, heat, vegetation, water, and appropriate economic-activity proxies. | Use product-specific quality masks, scale/offset, native support, collection/platform metadata, and temporal comparability. |
| ACLED, eligible UCDP products, and UN/humanitarian reports | Recorded exposure and corroborated disruption context. | Verify authorization, definitions, unique event IDs, precision, revisions, source lineage, and sensitive-use restrictions. |
| Financial providers, regulators, and authorized coverage/service records | Existing finance/payment/digital-service evidence. | Distinguish listing, operation, product terms, uptake, liquidity, network coverage, and actual service reliability. |

Public access will not be assumed to imply unrestricted redistribution. Sources requiring registration, permission, payment, or licensing will be flagged. A provider portal or a citation to a report will not be coded as an acquired analytical asset.

Relevant methodological and data entry points include [WFP MFI guidance](https://www.wfp.org/publications/market-functionality-index-mfi), [HDX Sudan](https://data.humdata.org/group/sdn), [IOM DTM Sudan](https://dtm.iom.int/sudan), [WorldPop](https://www.worldpop.org/), [FEWS NET Sudan](https://fews.net/east-africa/sudan), [UNOSAT](https://unosat.org/products), [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/sentinel-data/sentinel-2), [NASA Black Marble](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/black-marble), and [ACLED methodology](https://acleddata.com/methodology). These entry points identify source families; suitability and access will be established at the actual resource level.

### 5.3 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Sources will be included when they have an identifiable provenance, substantive relevance to an assessment question, interpretable geographic and temporal scope, and sufficient methodological information to support the proposed use. Older sources may be retained as baselines or historical evidence even when they are ineligible to describe current conditions.

Sources will be excluded from a particular analytical calculation when their units cannot be reconciled, geographic specificity is insufficient, duplication cannot be resolved, necessary observation dates are absent, permissions prevent the intended use, or methodological limitations make the indicator invalid. Exclusion from a calculation will not automatically mean deleting a potentially useful contextual source.

Unattributed images, unverified online assertions, copied statistics with no traceable origin, and AI-generated descriptions will not support factual findings. News or public social-media material may identify leads or corroborate a narrow dated claim after provenance and location checks; it will not be used as representative evidence of trader behavior or market-wide prevalence.

### 5.4 Extraction and Coding Procedure

Evidence will be extracted into structured records rather than stored only as narrative notes. The minimum observation record will contain:

| Record component | Required information |
|---|---|
| Identity | Observation ID; market ID if applicable; original source place name; geographic level. |
| Content | Indicator/question; original claim or value; unit; denominator; relevant category; short analyst interpretation. |
| Time | Observation start/end or date; publication date; acquisition date for imagery; retrieval date; applicable period. |
| Provenance | Original publisher; source/resource ID; URL or controlled internal reference; page/table reference; source lineage. |
| Evidence status | D/R/M/A/U class; source-quality dimensions; confidence class; missingness reason; contradictions. |
| Processing | Conversion or calculation; model/scenario/run ID; input references; method version. |
| Governance | License/use restriction; public/restricted classification; reviewer; approval status. |

Original text will be retained alongside translated summaries where terminology affects interpretation. Arabic place names, descriptions of operation, administrative labels, and terms such as market, station, square, or proposed site will be checked to avoid false matches. Ambiguous names will retain multiple candidates until resolved.

Numeric conversion will require a documented rule. Unverified sack weights, inconsistent retail/wholesale prices, mismatched commodity grades, or unspecified currencies will not be combined into a comparable series. Superseded records will be retained in the audit history rather than overwritten without explanation.

### 5.5 Geospatial Acquisition and Preparation

Acquired data will be preserved as immutable originals with metadata and file hashes where practicable. Working copies will be used for reprojection, cleaning, clipping, and joining. Administrative geography will be adopted before population reconciliation and reporting joins.

Market geolocation will require corroboration from independent existing sources and imagery where available. Market footprint, entrance, centroid, sub-site, and proposed intervention parcel will remain distinct objects. An approximate neighborhood point will not be silently promoted to an entrance suitable for detailed routing.

Local distance and area calculations will use a suitable projected coordinate system, normally WGS 84 / UTM zone 36N after checking the actual analysis extent. Longitude/latitude will be retained for exchange. Web Mercator display geometry will not determine analytical areas. National corridors may require geodesic distance or another appropriate regional method.

Raster processing will preserve the meaning of counts, densities, continuous measurements, and categorical classes. Population counts will be reallocated conservatively, not bilinearly interpolated and then summed as if mass were preserved. Coarse climate or nightlight information will not be represented as independent fine-resolution market observations merely because it is resampled.

### 5.6 Documentary Consultation Boundary

Requests to a custodian for an existing report, historical dataset, corrected document title, or already recorded market identifier will be treated as document acquisition or scope clarification. Such requests will not solicit new observations about market conditions. Any proposed request for a respondent's current perception, estimate, or newly observed status would cross into primary data collection and require a separate approved scope; it is not part of this methodology.

## 6. Indicator Framework

### 6.1 Indicator Design Rules

Indicators will be selected for decision relevance, methodological validity, geographic and temporal fit, feasibility of secondary acquisition, and an interpretable relationship to the question being assessed. Each indicator will have a definition, unit, valid denominator, source requirement, evidence class, eligibility conditions, direction where scored, and explicit limitation.

Indicators will be classified by use rather than assumed universally available:

- **Core descriptive indicators:** necessary to characterize the market or state why characterization is not possible.
- **Conditional quantitative indicators:** calculated only when specified evidence and denominator requirements are met.
- **Contextual indicators:** used to explain conditions at their actual scale, without attributing them to individual traders or markets.
- **Safeguard/feasibility indicators:** used to identify implementation conditions, not substitute for the required approval or clearance.

No baseline value, target, or achieved result will be invented to complete the framework. An indicator can remain unknown while the assessment still provides a valid account of the evidence gap and its implications.

### 6.6 Calculation and Reporting Controls

Every reported percentage will identify its numerator, denominator, geography, period, and observed-data coverage. Totals derived from joined tables will be checked for repeated markets, observations, events, or residents. Scenario results will not be added across mutually exclusive cases.

Composite scoring will use approved, fixed definitions and directions. Indicators such as economic activity, finance, inclusion, or food quality will not be forced into the composite merely because the data exist. Their primary role may be contextual interpretation, an intervention condition, or an evidence gap. Correlated components will be examined to avoid counting the same apparent signal repeatedly.

## 7. Proxy Indicators Replacing Field Survey Data

### 7.1 Substitution Principle

A proxy will be accepted only when its link to the intended concept is explicit, its input data are appropriate, and its limitations are stated. The assessment will distinguish a substitute measurement of a related physical condition from an indirect signal and from a concept for which no defensible secondary substitute exists.

The purpose is not to recreate every questionnaire response. It is to preserve the decisions the assessment needs to support while being honest about which observations cannot be reconstructed.

### 7.2 Field-to-Secondary Substitution Matrix

| Original field observation or question | Secondary substitute/proxy | What the substitute can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPS and market-site identification | Corroborated existing maps/records plus suitable imagery and an identity-resolution log. | A source-based location/footprint with recorded uncertainty. | A newly surveyed GPS fix, survey-grade boundary, ownership, or entrance accessibility without supporting evidence. |
| Buildings and shelter condition | Existing qualified damage analysis or adequate-resolution pre/post imagery. | Observable external physical change and visible condition within interpretable coverage. | Structural integrity, hidden defects, sanitation quality, internal damage, or explosive safety. |
| Open/closed status and trading frequency | Dated market-specific monitoring or existing administrative/assessment records. | Reported operating status for the documented period. | Current hours or trading frequency if the source does not record them; operation from an intact roof. |
| Footfall and population served | Population scenarios, network reach, and unique allocation among modelled candidate markets. | Potential physically reachable resident exposure. | Actual visitors, purchases, customer loyalty, effective demand, or unique beneficiaries. |
| Transport time and distance | Mode-specific network travel-time model with verified entrances and scenario assumptions. | Conditional physical travel burden, disconnection, and detour. | Observed journey time, fares, safety, waiting, checkpoint passage, or universal accessibility. |
| Goods available and product diversity | Explicit existing availability observations using a type-appropriate basket. | Documented assortment and availability for monitored items/periods. | Stock volumes, duration of stockouts, food safety, or complete assortment from a price list alone. |
| Prices and price stability | Harmonized existing item-market price panels. | Comparable documented price movements and conditional volatility measures. | Seller margins, profiteering, willingness to pay, real purchasing power without a denominator, or competition structure. |
| Supply origins and transport routes | Dated supply-chain reports with separately modelled route alternatives. | Documented origins/connections and conditional network dependency. | Current tonnage, supplier shares, route passability, or transport mode shares without records. |
| Business activity | Documented operation and enterprise records, with neighborhood lights/POIs used only as contextual signals. | Supported activity evidence or a qualified neighborhood pattern. | Sales, profits, employment, market GDP, vendor numbers, or the cause of an observed change. |
| Access to finance and payments | Existing service-operation records and dated product terms, supplemented by mapped presence. | Documented services and institutional constraints. | Actual approval, affordability, borrowing, liquidity, insurance coverage, or equal access from proximity. |
| Women/youth/disability participation | Existing applicable disaggregated market/enterprise studies. | Source-defined participation or constraints within that study's scope. | Representative market shares or preferences derived from resident age/sex data or inferred disability. |
| Competition and trader concentration | Existing firm ownership/sales/market-share evidence and documented entry barriers. | A scoped documentary finding if the underlying evidence is adequate. | A concentration ratio or monopoly finding from shop density or similar prices alone. |
| Inventory and continuity planning | Existing stock/enterprise records or published business assessments. | The recorded inventory/continuity condition where applicable. | Inventory days, reserve capital, insurance, or emergency finance from storage footprint. |
| Flood and environmental conditions | Global/observed flood products, climate/surface context, and existing utility/environmental records. | Exposure screening and conditional design concerns. | Plot-level flood depth certainty, drinking-water quality, working drainage, or environmental approval. |
| Security and safe access | Reviewed recorded events and existing specialist operational/clearance records. | Recorded exposure and documented requirements/status within the source's scope. | Absence of coercion, universal safe access, or clearance from event counts. |
| Rehabilitation priorities and costs | Evidenced deficits, scenario comparison, existing feasibility records, and explicit decision criteria. | A conditional option/shortlist and documentation of prerequisites. | Procurement-ready quantities, current tender costs, consent, or implementation authorization without required records. |

### 7.3 Core Proxy Calculations

**Potential reachable residents.** For market $m$, origin cell $g$, and scenario $s$:

$$P_{m,s}=\sum_g N_{g,s}\,I(t_{gm,s}\leq60)\,I(m=j^*_{g,s}),$$

where $N_{g,s}$ is the scenario population, $t_{gm,s}$ is modelled walking time in minutes, and $j^*_{g,s}$ is the eligible candidate market with minimum feasible travel time. Ties will be resolved deterministically, and unreachable/unallocated origins will remain explicit. This is a unique allocation among the specified candidates, not an empirical consumer-choice model.

**Documented basket availability and completeness.** Let $a$ be explicitly available eligible items, $o$ items with valid availability observations, and $b$ all pre-declared eligible items:

$$V=100a/o,\qquad Q_b=100o/b.$$

The availability fraction will be undefined if $o=0$; completeness will be reported with the value. The proposed composite eligibility rule is $Q_b\geq80\%$. This threshold is an analytical choice requiring approval, not a universal humanitarian standard or protection against all monitoring bias.

**Visible damage and observable coverage.** For qualifying damaged structures $d$, observable baseline structures $v$, and identified baseline structures $n$:

$$D=100d/v,\qquad Q_v=100v/n.$$

If $v=0$ or the baseline is invalid, damage will not be calculated. A proposed 80% observable-coverage rule applies before using the visible-condition component in composite screening; the remaining uncertainty and potential selection bias will still be disclosed.

**Route disruption.** Where both baseline and alternative travel times are valid:

$$\mathrm{DetourRatio}=t_{\mathrm{disruption}}/t_{\mathrm{reference}}.$$

If a destination becomes unreachable, the result will be classified as disconnected rather than assigned a finite ratio. Neither the ratio nor the existence of an alternative path establishes safe or affordable transport.

**Contextual activity change.** A percentage change in nightlight radiance will be calculated only when the baseline is valid, positive, and above the relevant product-specific noise floor. Otherwise absolute difference and quality information will be reported. The result will remain a radiance statistic, not an economic-output estimate.

### 7.4 Proxy Acceptance Test

Before use, each proxy will be reviewed against five questions: whether the intended concept is defined; whether the spatial/temporal scale is appropriate; whether major alternative explanations are addressed; whether the uncertainty is reported; and whether a defensible secondary corroboration exists. A proxy failing a critical test will be downgraded to contextual use or excluded from the relevant conclusion or score. There will be no requirement to invent a substitute for every unavailable field variable.

## 8. Data Validation Methodology

### 8.1 Validation Strategy

Validation will be embedded in acquisition, extraction, calculation, interpretation, and release. It will distinguish source credibility, observation validity, processing accuracy, model plausibility, and decision eligibility. Agreement among model outputs will not be treated as ground truth, and independent desk review will not be described as field validation.

### 8.2 Source Appraisal and Confidence

Each observation will be assessed on four dimensions, scored 0, 0.5, or 1 using recorded reasons:

| Dimension | Score 1 | Score 0.5 | Score 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reliability | Identifiable accountable source with a relevant method and traceable provenance. | Identifiable source with partial methodological/provenance information. | Provenance unreliable or inadequate for the proposed claim. |
| Spatial specificity | Directly matches the market/area required by the indicator. | Supports a coarser contextual interpretation or an explicitly qualified partial match. | Geographic attribution cannot support the intended claim. |
| Temporal relevance | Observation period known and appropriate to the indicator/comparison. | Known older or partly mismatched period useful with qualification. | Date is unknown or incompatible with the intended current/comparative use. |
| Method transparency | Definitions, methods, units, and denominator are sufficiently described. | Some methodological detail is available but material limitations remain. | Measurement meaning cannot be established for the proposed use. |

The internal quality index will be:

$$Q=(q_r+q_s+q_t+q_m)/4.$$

High confidence requires $Q\geq0.8$, no dimension scored zero, and no unresolved material contradiction. Medium confidence requires $Q\geq0.5$ without a critical validity failure and without meeting the high-confidence rule. Low confidence applies below 0.5, or where a material spatial/temporal or methodological limitation prevents stronger interpretation. No defensible estimate will be coded unknown, not assigned a low numeric performance value.

Critical validity failures will override an otherwise acceptable average: for example, a highly credible state-level statistic will not become a current market observation. Confidence describes the supported use of the evidence, not the prestige of its publisher, and is not a statistical probability.

### 8.3 Triangulation and Contradiction Resolution

Strong current-condition findings will seek at least two genuinely independent and compatible evidence lines. Independence will be checked through original-source lineage; multiple reposts of one report will count as one source chain. An image and a damage map derived from that same image will not automatically constitute independent validation.

Where only one applicable source exists, a qualified single-source statement may be used if the relevant eligibility conditions are satisfied. It will be labelled accordingly and will not be elevated to corroborated evidence. Absence of a second source is not grounds to fabricate corroboration.

Contradictions will be examined for differences in dates, market sections, definitions, population groups, monitoring methods, and spatial support. The assessment will retain both claims, document the resolution, and explain why one may be more applicable. Unresolved material disagreement will result in a conflicting/unknown status or an explicit range, not an undocumented average of incompatible claims.

### 8.4 Data Integrity and Geospatial Checks

| Validation area | Required checks | Action if the check fails |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and joins | Unique market IDs; aliases mapped correctly; separate markets not merged; sub-sites linked to parent; administrative join reviewed. | Quarantine disputed identity/geometry from location-dependent analysis. |
| Coordinates and geometry | Longitude/latitude order; plausible extent; valid geometries; appropriate CRS; duplicate footprints and slivers reviewed. | Correct working data with an audit trail or retain location uncertainty. |
| Network topology | True junctions; bridge/tunnel separation; mode and directional restrictions; no false river crossing; entrance snaps reviewed. | Repair supported topology; otherwise flag affected routes/results and test alternatives. |
| Population | Counts/density distinction; mass-preserving allocation; compatible totals; no stock/flow double counting; assigned plus unassigned closure. | Withhold current-resident totals or label as baseline/scenario until reconciliation is defensible. |
| Imagery and damage | Dates, registration, sensor/resolution, shadows/clouds, baseline inventory, observable denominator, and reviewer agreement. | Suppress unsupported structure-level estimates; use coarser or documentary evidence. |
| Prices and availability | Comparable units/grades/currencies; duplicate observations; missingness; valid denominators; sufficient series length. | Exclude incompatible comparisons; retain descriptive observations with limitations. |
| Conflict records | Event ID uniqueness; category choice; date/location precision; revisions and source overlap. | Remove from fine-scale calculation where unsuitable while retaining appropriate contextual summaries. |
| Environmental data | Correct global versus regional product; depth/temperature/rainfall units; quality flags; nodata; permanent water; temporal relevance. | Correct product/processing or withhold the affected exposure result. |
| Aggregation | Unique people/markets/events; no overlap inflation; no addition across alternative scenarios/periods. | Recalculate on valid keys or use union-based summaries. |
| Publication | Source traceability, permissions, sensitivity, classification, geographic/temporal labels, and reviewer approval. | Hold release of affected findings or produce an appropriately generalized/qualified alternative. |

### 8.5 Independent Desk Review

A reviewer other than the original analyst will examine every market considered for a rehabilitation shortlist and at least 20% of other interpreted sites, or all sites when fewer than five remain outside the shortlist. The non-shortlist selection will include difficult/low-confidence cases and a documented random component where practical. The review will cover source interpretation, geolocation, image classification, indicator eligibility, and consistency of recommendations.

All material disagreements will be logged with the original interpretation, reviewer finding, final adjudication, evidence, and responsible approver. Image-classification agreement may be reported, but will be described as agreement between desk interpreters rather than measured real-world damage accuracy. Sampling for review is a quality-control procedure, not a representative survey of markets or traders.

### 8.6 Missing Data and Uncertainty

Missing values will retain a reason: not reported, stale, not applicable, inaccessible, conflicting, unsuitable scale, or insufficient coverage. Zero will mean a supported zero measurement or valid model result, not a missing value.

For weighted composite components $k$ with fixed weights $w_k$, unknown component scores will be represented by uncertainty bounds rather than available-weight renormalization:

$$S_m^- = \sum_{k\in K_m}w_k s_{mk},\qquad S_m^+ = S_m^-+100\sum_{k\notin K_m}w_k.$$

Here $K_m$ contains eligible known components. A zero lower bound for an unknown component is a bounding device, not an assertion that the market performs poorly. Weighted evidence coverage will be reported separately. A midpoint will not be presented as an observed score.

Complete comparable scoring will require all required components to be eligible. Provisional screening will follow the pre-declared coverage rules, including minimum coverage within each criterion, rather than relying only on an overall percentage. Insufficient-evidence cases will receive no point rank. Statistical intervals supplied by original sources will be distinguished from scenario ranges, which will not be labelled confidence intervals.

### 8.7 Sensitivity and Plausibility Testing

The assessment will test population cases, alternative eligible population products, walking speeds, known/unknown route assumptions, wet-season disruption, selected bridge closure, conflict-event windows/buffers, normalization anchors, and relative weight changes. The modelled baseline will not be described as verified observed behavior.

Sensitivity results will report changes in potential reach, disconnection, score bounds, shortlist membership, and rank bands where ranks are eligible. A plus/minus 20% relative weight perturbation will be distinguished from a 20-percentage-point change. Alternative weight sets will be labelled policy scenarios, not silently replace the agreed base.

Where comparable source observations exist, model estimates may be checked against them, retaining differences in dates and definitions. Expert desk plausibility review may identify processing errors or unrealistic assumptions, but will not validate actual travel time or structural condition in the absence of observations.

### 8.8 Release Criteria

A finding will be released only when its provenance is traceable, the variable is valid for its claimed scale and period, denominator and uncertainty are visible, licensing and sensitivity conditions are satisfied, and review has addressed material errors or contradictions. Recommendations will additionally identify dependencies and unanswered questions.

No investment-ready designation will be issued where identity, authorized security/access and explosive-remnant requirements, tenure/permission, relevant technical/environmental-social conditions, or economic/O&M prerequisites remain failed or unknown. Those conditions are separate from the weighted score and cannot be compensated by a larger catchment or higher economic significance.

## 9. Limitations and Assumptions

### 9.1 Principal Limitations and Mitigation

| Limitation | Potential consequence | Methodological response and residual constraint |
|---|---|---|
| No primary data collection | Unobserved behavior, preferences, current trader constraints, and satisfaction cannot be established directly. | Use applicable existing evidence and qualified proxies; leave unsupported concepts unknown. This limitation cannot be removed through more detailed mapping alone. |
| Purposive and unresolved cohort | Selected markets may not represent all state markets or locality coverage. | Confirm identities/scope and disclose exclusions; avoid state-wide prevalence or representative survey claims. |
| Conflict-related access and reporting bias | Better-connected or more visible places may have more information than highly affected areas. | Map evidence coverage, retain low-data populations in the need portfolio, and do not equate weak documentation with low need. |
| Rapid change and heterogeneous observation dates | A technically correct historic statement may be misleading as a current finding. | Preserve dates, apply freshness alerts, refresh after material changes, and use historical labels. No static product guarantees operational currency. |
| Population movement and weak denominators | Catchments may misstate resident presence or double count displaced/returning people. | Reconcile definitions and dates; use conservative scenarios; report baseline exposure where current totals cannot be established. |
| Geolocation and network incompleteness | Misidentified markets, false connections, or omitted roads may distort access results. | Corroborate identities, review barriers/topology, retain approximate/disputed classes, and test affected scenarios. |
| Image resolution and visibility | Small stalls, interior damage, temporary shelters, and obstructions may be unobservable. | Use suitable products and observable denominators; suppress unsupported fine-scale estimates. |
| Economic-proxy ambiguity | Lights or mapped POIs may be mistaken for business recovery. | Restrict interpretation to the supported neighborhood signal or mapped presence; require documentary corroboration for stronger economic claims. |
| Sparse or incompatible commodity monitoring | Apparent shortages, diversity differences, or price movements may reflect sample coverage or units. | Predefine baskets and completeness; harmonize eligible series; avoid inventing observations or treating missing rows as stockouts. |
| Weak inclusion/competition data | Trader shares, social barriers, ownership, or power relations may remain uncertain. | Retain source-specific qualitative findings; do not infer from demography, footprint, or business count alone. |
| Global hazard-model limitations | Exposure may omit local drainage, defenses, terrain alterations, or recent events. | Separate modelled and observed evidence; retain site-specific investigation as an implementation requirement. |
| Licensing, sensitivity, and institutional restrictions | Some assets may be unavailable or unsuitable for public release. | Maintain an access/reuse register; use cleared alternatives; omit or generalize restricted content lawfully. |
| No causal counterfactual | Before/after patterns cannot establish intervention impact. | Describe association and temporal change; do not claim attributable income, jobs, or recovery. |
| Incomplete feasibility records | A plausible option may lack approval, engineering evidence, operating finance, or lawful land access. | Keep the option conditional and identify the enabling due diligence; do not substitute a high score for a missing requirement. |

### 9.2 Assumptions Register

Assumptions will be recorded with a rationale, source if any, approval status, sensitivity test, and the decision affected. They will not be written as observed facts.

| Assumption | Proposed treatment | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative geography | Adopt one specified boundary/version and stable locality identifiers. | Source labels may differ; spatial joins and disputes require review. |
| Baseline comparability | Use seasonally matched pre-conflict observations where possible. | Differences may include product vintage, urban development, or observation method, not just conflict effects. |
| Central walking speed | Illustrative 4 km/h, with slower 2.5 km/h and faster 5 km/h sensitivity cases. | Not observed local behavior or an adequate disability-access model. |
| Service thresholds | 15/30/60-minute walking thresholds for comparative planning. | Not established travel preferences or universal access standards. |
| Neighborhood definition | Fixed 5 km terrestrial neighborhood for the agreed local-burden statistic. | Results depend on this analytical support and must not be confused with the full consumer catchment. |
| Population scenarios | Compatible low/central/high resident distributions where defensible. | Scenarios are not remotely counted people or probabilities of return. |
| Market-choice allocation | Nearest feasible eligible market, with deterministic tie-breaking. | Omits preference, prices, social access, specialization, and possibly competing markets outside the cohort. |
| Availability/visibility completeness | Proposed 80% observation/observable-coverage thresholds for relevant composite inputs. | Thresholds require approval and do not remove bias within the observed portion. |
| Freshness alerts | Review security/road records older than 30 days, market status/prices older than 90 days, and current-condition imagery older than 180 days. | These are screening alerts, not guarantees that newer evidence is current, safe, or valid. Static geography/climatology follows different version checks. |
| Policy weights | Retain the requested six-criterion weighting in the base model. | Weights express a decision preference, not empirically demonstrated causal contributions. |
| Recovery setting | Treat locations as conflict-affected recovery environments unless dated evidence supports a more specific label. | Do not assume uniform post-conflict conditions, safe return, or sustainable reintegration. |

### 9.3 Interpretation Safeguards

The assessment will not assume that return demonstrates voluntary or sustainable recovery; that apparently vacant land is available for construction; that a mapped facility operates; that low recorded conflict exposure means safety; that more covered floor area guarantees greater use; or that a large nearby population implies adequate purchasing power.

Rehabilitation options will be screened for the risk of excluding informal vendors, concentrating benefits among already advantaged businesses, imposing unaffordable fees, increasing debt without demand, or creating water/energy/maintenance liabilities. These risks will be examined from existing evidence and retained as unresolved safeguards where the secondary-only design cannot establish the necessary conditions.

| Defined assessment universe | Confirmed markets, original names/aliases, scope decisions, market types, locality assignments, geometry status, and excluded/unresolved entries. | No invented market or location; coverage claims match the actual cohort. |
| Market-specific assessment profiles | Dated evidence on role, operation, physical condition, services, goods/prices, potential reach, supply links, constraints, risks, and conditional responses. | Unknowns, geographic support, confidence, and evidence sources remain explicit. |
| Comparative market-system synthesis | Common constraints and differentiated roles; bottlenecks; supply dependencies; service and institutional issues; implications for livelihood recovery. | Does not generalize beyond the evidence or treat unlike markets as directly equivalent. |
| Accessibility and population findings | Mode/scenario-specific travel burden, service reach, unique potential resident ranges, unallocated origins, and disruption sensitivity. | Totals reconcile, overlap is controlled, and outputs are not labelled observed customers or safe journeys. |
| Infrastructure and environmental screening | Observable-condition results and coverage; separate hazard/event exposure; service and environmental constraints. | No unsupported structure-level damage, engineering safety, water-quality, or site-suitability claim. |
| Economic and livelihood appraisal | Evidence-supported business/service conditions, purchasing-power context where available, inclusive opportunity pathways, and constraints requiring complementary measures. | No fabricated jobs, trader counts, incomes, participation rates, or turnover estimates. |
| Conditional rehabilitation options | Evidenced problem, no-build/service alternatives, dependencies, potential reach, applicable safeguards, O&M requirements, and required preparation. | Option language distinguishes proposed, feasible, approved, and implemented status. |
| Prioritization contribution | Eligible criterion inputs, need/readiness distinctions, uncertainty bounds, sensitivity results, and gate status. | No point ranking from insufficient evidence; high-need/blocked markets remain visible. |

### 10.2 Evidence and Reproducibility Outputs

The assessment will produce a searchable source register; an observation/evidence ledger; an indicator dictionary; a market identity/alias register; acquisition and license records; a source-lineage and contradiction log; a methods/assumptions register; and a review/adjudication record. Geospatial results will retain input references, coordinate systems, tool/model versions, parameter values, scenario definitions, processing dates, and reviewer approval.

An evidence-gap matrix will identify the unanswered question, affected market/group, reason for the gap, decision consequence, existing-source acquisition option if available, and any due diligence that must remain outside the secondary-only assessment. The matrix will distinguish a remediable processing gap from a fundamental limit of remote or documentary inference.

The final report will include a concise evidence-strength statement for each principal conclusion, explaining what is established, what is modelled, what remains assumed, and what cannot yet be concluded. This will allow donor and program reviewers to trace recommendations without requiring them to interpret raw GIS files.

### 10.3 Contribution to Subsequent Products

Validated findings and cleared spatial outputs will provide the analytical foundation for subsequent maps, dashboard indicators, market scorecards, and the ArcGIS StoryMap. Those communication products will inherit the same dates, units, evidence classes, confidentiality restrictions, and uncertainty rules; visualization will not upgrade an assumption into evidence.

The methodology will be considered fully implemented only after the actual source acquisition, analytical processing, validation, and review procedures have been completed and documented. Where evidence remains insufficient, a transparent and decision-specific statement of uncertainty will be an expected assessment output, not replaced by an apparently complete but unsupported finding.
