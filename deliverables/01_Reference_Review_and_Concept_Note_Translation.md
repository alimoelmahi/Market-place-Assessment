## 2. Faithful Working English Translation of REF03

### Survey to Assess the Locations, Infrastructure, and Functions of Markets in Khartoum State

### Introduction

The war that broke out in Sudan in April 2023 caused major economic and social impacts, including forcing millions of Sudanese people to flee. It also caused extensive destruction of basic community infrastructure across large parts of the country, including public and private facilities such as hospitals, schools, water and electricity facilities, government offices, and residential buildings. Many public and private properties were also looted and destroyed, depriving millions of Sudanese people of access to their basic needs.

Khartoum State, formerly an important urban center, the capital of Sudan, and home to approximately one quarter of the country's population before the war, is among the states most affected. Most vital facilities were subject to destruction, looting, and vandalism. Markets were among the most important facilities damaged during the war. This included the destruction and burning of buildings, shops, and warehouses; damage to and theft of goods, traders' property, and stocks; interruption of water, electricity, cleaning, and environmental sanitation services; accumulation of rubble, waste, and remnants of war; displacement of traders; and loss of their capital and employment.

Following improvement in security conditions in Khartoum State and the beginning of displaced people's return to their areas of origin, the need emerged to provide substantial assistance to enable them to rebuild what the war had destroyed. UNDP therefore seeks to make substantial efforts to support the Government of Sudan's efforts to create conditions for displaced people's return to Khartoum State and provide the requirements for a dignified life, including basic services and livelihood opportunities. In this context, UNDP is assessing the infrastructure and functions of Khartoum State's markets to identify the needs for rehabilitating market environments and restoring activity, enabling markets to provide employment and livelihoods for thousands of traders and workers, supply essential goods and basic needs to residents, and provide marketing opportunities for producers.

### Assessment Objectives

The assessment aims to establish the current situation of markets in relation to:

1. Infrastructure condition, level of activity, and analysis of demand for products among people using the selected markets.
2. The capacity of traders, particularly young people and women, to meet demand for products.
3. Availability of financial services for market participants, including finance, payment instruments, and insurance; identification of the type and scale of assistance required; and development of proposals to help provide that assistance.
4. Identification of risks faced by market participants, with proposals for managing and reducing those risks.

### Methodology and Data Collection Tools

The assessment of market environments and functions relies on organized quantitative and qualitative data collected using a dedicated form, completed through interviews with a purposively selected sample of five participants per market. Participants include traders, workers in market facilities, and regulatory authorities responsible for market organization and management.

Qualitative information is also collected through photographic documentation of market sites, structures, and facilities.

The assessment covers a sample of 15 markets in Khartoum State, intended to cover all state localities and different market types.

## 3. Extracted Results Logic and Adaptation

| Concept-note objective | Observable secondary indicator | What cannot be inferred | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure and activity | Dated damage evidence; roofed-space footprint; documented utilities; matched-season activity proxies | Structural integrity, opening hours, sales, footfall, or food hygiene from medium-resolution imagery | Report physical-condition evidence separately from operating status. |
| Product demand | Scenario-based reachable population; published basket prices and availability | Household preferences, willingness to pay, expenditure, or trader sales | Label population as potential catchment, not customers or effective purchasing power. |
| Women/youth trader capacity | Existing sex/age-disaggregated assessments and program records where relevant | Numbers, shares, profitability, constraints, or preferences from buildings or population age structure | Preserve unknowns and add inclusive-design safeguards, not fabricated participation estimates. |
| Finance, payment, insurance | Dated branch/agent operation reports; documented product terms | Credit approval, affordability, liquidity, women's equal access, or insurance uptake from a mapped branch | Distinguish mapped financial presence from verified service availability. |
| Market risks | Documented disruptions; conflict exposure; flood and heat screening | Safe passage, explosive-ordnance clearance, absence of abuse, or site-level engineering safety | Require separate investment safeguards and current operational review. |
| Assistance type and scale | Evidenced infrastructure deficits and scenario intervention packages | Tender-ready costs or proven additional income/jobs without designs and baseline data | Prepare pre-feasibility packages with explicit conditions and cost-estimation requirements. |

No numerical targets, budget, outcome baseline, indicator definitions, or rehabilitation quantities are prescribed by REF03. The planned five interviews per market would imply 75 interviews if 15 sites were confirmed; these are planned observations, not completed records. This package replaces new interviews and new photography with documentary extraction and remote analysis only. An interview by telephone or online would still be primary research and is outside scope.

## 4. Assumptions Requiring Verification

1. The claim that Khartoum State hosted approximately one quarter of Sudan's pre-war population is translated faithfully but is not adopted as a demographic statistic. Verify denominator, year, geography, and source.
2. General claims of improved security and return are time- and location-dependent. Return is not proof of voluntary, safe, dignified, or sustainable reintegration.
3. The note's claim of coverage across all localities is an intention, not demonstrated by the market list. Join confirmed locations to one adopted administrative layer before assessing coverage.
4. The intended sample is purposive and not statistically representative. Neither the original plan nor the secondary-data replacement supports state-wide prevalence estimates.
5. Prior capital loss and displacement may constrain supply even where buildings appear intact. Rehabilitation alone is not proof of livelihood recovery.
