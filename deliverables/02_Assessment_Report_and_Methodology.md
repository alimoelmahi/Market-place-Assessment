# Khartoum State Markets: Secondary-Evidence Assessment and Methodology

Decision-support design and preliminary documentary assessment | Version 1.0 | 27 October 2026

## Executive Assessment

Market rehabilitation should be screened as a combined infrastructure, supply-chain, services, and livelihoods intervention. A rebuilt shelter will not restore trading if suppliers cannot replenish stocks, customers lack purchasing power, vendors cannot use the chosen location, finance is unavailable, or land and protection issues remain unresolved.

The supplied documents support a focused recovery rationale, a broad assessment framework, and three illustrative market profiles. They do not establish current conditions across all major markets in Khartoum State. The concept note describes 15 markets but supplies 14 identifiable entries; several names and administrative assignments are unresolved. No verified market geometries, downloaded analytical rasters, cleaned price panel, or completed engineering records are included in this package. Consequently, no actual market scores, population-served totals, damage percentages, travel times, construction costs, or priority ranking are asserted.

**Recommended decision:** approve a secondary-evidence pre-feasibility assessment with a reproducible GIS pipeline, explicit confidence and temporal controls, and a conditional investment shortlist. Do not promise equivalence to field-based evidence for trader behavior, protection, engineering safety, competition, sales, or preferences. The quality standard can be donor-grade in transparency and reproducibility; the scope of inference cannot be identical to a field study.

The attached benchmark reports shelter disuse, roadside trading, interrupted construction, and land-allocation issues at Hilat Kuku, Soug 6, and Umdawan Ban. These are attributed documentary signals, not newly verified observations. Their main analytical lesson is that site placement, transport connections, vendor incentives, tenure, and maintenance matter as much as built capacity. Detailed claims and qualifications appear in the reference review.

## 1. Purpose, Decisions, and Assessment Boundary

### 1.1 Decisions supported

1. Identify markets for further rehabilitation preparation, distinguishing high need from implementable opportunities.
2. Locate population-access gaps, disrupted supply connections, and cross-market dependencies.
3. Identify no-regrets design features and conditional livelihood packages.
4. Sequence quick enabling measures, medium-term rehabilitation, and long-term recovery investments.
5. Establish a refreshable spatial evidence baseline for monitoring, not a causal impact evaluation.

Not supported without additional evidence: allocation of individual grants; trader-level eligibility; authorization of cash transfers; certification of safe routes or cleared land; structural safety certification; tender-ready quantities; or prediction of jobs and incomes.

### 1.2 Geography and unit of analysis

- Primary geography: the adopted Khartoum State boundary and its verified localities. Analyze Khartoum, Jabal Awlia, Omdurman, Ombada, Karari, Bahri, and Sharg El Neel against the selected boundary version; confirm official names and PCodes before use.
- Unit: a market site with a stable ID, linked to entrances, bounded footprint, optional east/west sub-sites, nearby services, and a dated market-period record.
- Functional geography: extend accessibility analysis beyond the state boundary and supply-chain mapping to relevant national production areas, ports, border nodes, and intermediate hubs. Do not clip routes at the state border.
- Initial cohort: 14 provisional entries transcribed from REF03. Resolve the fifteenth entry; do not make up a fifteenth site. A broader inventory of major markets requires a separately approved expansion of scope.
- Market hierarchy: retail neighborhood, district mixed, metropolitan wholesale, and specialized livestock/fish/logistics nodes. Assign from dated documentary evidence; never infer commercial turnover from rank or footprint alone.
- Market classes must remain distinguishable. Use a core food basket only for comparable food markets, and separate specialized livestock/fish measures. Do not reward a fish market for stocking cereals or penalize it for not doing so.

### 1.3 Time design

Set a signed analysis date and retain the extraction date separately. This design's source-review cutoff is 27 October 2026, not a statement that all source observations are current to that day.

| Period | Proposed definition | Use and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-conflict baseline | Matched months in 2022 and, where available, January-March 2023; all dates before 15 April 2023 | Anchor infrastructure and activity comparisons; avoid a baseline contaminated by conflict onset. |
| Disruption phase | Dated observations from April 2023 onward, split into documented episodes | Do not select only the worst image and call it a uniform conflict period. |
| Latest comparable period | Latest complete, quality-controlled month or quarter available by cutoff | Each layer retains its own observation window; no mixed-date map labeled simply "2026 current." |
| Seasonality | Dry/wet and matched calendar-month comparisons | Separate rainfall, harvest, holidays, sensor conditions, and reporting gaps from recovery signals. |
| Conflict windows | Last 90 and 180 days ending at analysis date; 365-day contextual view | Reporting latency and location precision remain visible; absence of reports is not safety. |
| Population scenarios | Latest harmonized low/central/high resident estimates with explicit reference date | Return and displacement changes are modeled scenarios, not remotely counted residents. |

Default freshness alerts, proposed rather than UNDP standards: security and road-status evidence older than 30 days is stale for operational screening; market prices/status older than 90 days is stale for the current market profile; imagery older than 180 days is historical for present-condition interpretation. Hazard climatologies and boundary layers follow version checks rather than these windows. Even evidence within a freshness threshold cannot certify safe access. Older material stays available as historical evidence.

## 2. Standards and Analytical Principles

Use REF01 as a thematic questionnaire crosswalk, WFP's Market Functionality Index (MFI) as a dimensional reference, Sphere's market-aware response principles, and established emergency market-system analysis principles such as EMMA and ICRC/IFRC rapid market assessment. This is an adapted secondary-evidence framework, not the corporate WFP MFI or a claimed UNDP-approved index.

The WFP MFI publication page reviewed for this package identifies nine dimensions: assortment, availability, price, supply-chain resilience, competition, infrastructure, services, food quality, and access/protection [S09]. Food quality and protection cannot be certified by the proposed imagery proxies. WFP's index and the seven-item functionality section of REF01 are not interchangeable.

Apply proportionality, do-no-harm, conflict sensitivity, inclusion, environmental and social screening, and transparent alternatives analysis. Rehabilitation must not facilitate forced return, displacement of informal traders, inequitable land claims, or exclusion of women, young people, persons with disabilities, or minority groups.

### 2.1 Evidence taxonomy

Every indicator observation carries one of these mutually exclusive evidence types:

| Code | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| D | Directly documented in an existing source | A dated published market report describes a closure; this assessment did not observe it directly. |
| R | Remotely observed physical feature/change | An interpretable roof change in dated, adequate-resolution imagery. |
| M | Modeled estimate or proxy | Travel time, adjusted population allocation, catchment, centrality, or nightlight-based signal. |
| A | Explicit analytical assumption | Walking speed or an unverified bridge scenario. |
| U | Unknown / not supportable | Current number of women traders without an applicable source. |

Store the source claim separately from the analyst interpretation. A source may be credible while a specific claim is stale, geographically coarse, or uncertain. A country's reported supply shortage is not a documented shortage at every Khartoum market. Different agencies repeating one original report are one evidence lineage, not independent triangulation.

### 2.2 Confidence and contradictions

Score source reliability, spatial specificity, temporal relevance, and methodological transparency on 0, 0.5, or 1. Use their arithmetic mean as an internal evidence-quality index, not a statistical probability. High: at least 0.8, no component zero, no unresolved material contradiction; medium: at least 0.5 without meeting high; low: below 0.5 or any critical specificity/date failure. Unknown means no defensible estimate and is not assigned a confidence score.

For quantitative uncertainty report source-provided intervals where available; otherwise disclose named scenario ranges. Do not describe scenario bounds as 95% confidence intervals. Require two genuinely independent lines of evidence for strong current-condition statements; when that is impossible, use a qualified single-source statement. Keep imagery interpretation agreement separate from real-world accuracy. An independent desk reviewer should examine all shortlisted sites and at least 20% of other interpreted sites, resolving disagreements in a decision log.

Contradictory claims stay in the evidence table with dates, geography, original wording, and adjudication. Prefer a more specific, recent, methodologically transparent observation when justified, but do not overwrite disagreement. Use `unknown_conflicting` when conflict cannot be resolved.

## 3. Secondary Data Collection Strategy

### 3.1 Search and acquisition protocol

1. Create a source register with publisher, title, URL, publication and observation dates, geography, methods, license, access conditions, and retrieval status.
2. Search English and Arabic name variants plus Khartoum, Bahri, Omdurman, locality names, market, prices, damage, reopening, roads, returns, floods, and livelihoods. Use official UN/partner repositories, HDX, DTM, WFP, FEWS NET, UNOSAT, FAO, World Bank, UNDP, government publications, and independently reviewed research first.
3. Search pre-conflict baselines separately from post-April-2023 documents. Record query, repository, date range, inclusion/exclusion decision, and rejected duplicates. Include credible Arabic sources, not only English reports.
4. Include sources with identifiable publisher, relevant geography/period, and interpretable methods; use press and social media only as dated leads or corroboration after geolocation and provenance checks. Never infer absence of trading from no online posts.
5. Download permitted datasets and preserve immutable originals, file hashes, full metadata, and resource-level URLs. A portal being reachable does not establish that a Sudan file was downloaded or is fit for use.
6. Extract observations into long-form tables. Do not scrape private groups, collect trader identities, contact new respondents, or substitute AI-generated descriptions for evidence.
7. Georeference and cross-check market names against at least two independent secondary sources and imagery where possible. Record uncertainty, aliases, and rejected matches.
8. Apply quality and licensing gates before processing or publication. Restricted-access data may inform an internal product only within its terms.

The source catalogue includes datasets that are candidates rather than obtained assets. ACLED, GSMA, commercial imagery, and some historical archives may require accounts, approval, or fees; they are not unconditionally open data. GADM redistribution/commercial use requires special attention [S15]. Keep a viable public-data route through COD, OSM, Copernicus, WorldPop, NASA, and public reports, subject to resource-level checks.

### 3.2 Source hierarchy and substitution limits

| Evidence need | Preferred source and method | Substitute for original survey | Residual limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site identity and roles | Local authority publications, REF03, OSM, dated institutional maps | Market name, type, location | A gazetteer point may identify a neighborhood, not an entrance or bounded market. |
| Current operation | Dated market-specific assessment plus independent corroboration | Reported open/partial/closed state | No reliable opening hours, transaction volume, or trading frequency without records. |
| Physical condition | Existing UNOSAT analysis; licensed dated very-high-resolution imagery | Visible damage/roof coverage and obstruction screening | Cannot inspect electrical wiring, foundations, internal damage, hygiene, or hidden ordnance. |
| Demand | WorldPop/HRSL baseline, harmonized DTM/COD totals, price and food-security context | Potential reachable residents and needs context | Not household purchasing power, preferences, actual use, or market turnover. |
| Products and prices | WFP/FEWS NET/FAO/IFPRI and applicable published market monitoring | Documented basket coverage and price movements | A price quote is not inventory or stockout proof; state-level prices are contextual only. |
| Trader capacity and finance | Existing enterprise assessments, branch/agent status reports, official product terms | Documented services and constraints | POI density cannot establish liquidity, credit access, ownership, or trader demographics. |
| Supply network | Published trade maps, transport assessments, independently reviewed route reports | Reported corridors and modeled alternatives | No trade tonnage, supplier count, or route passability from shortest paths alone. |
| Security and environment | ACLED/UCDP plus UN reporting; JRC/UNOSAT/Sentinel flood evidence | Recorded exposure and hazard screening | Not safe access, attack probability, micro-drainage design, or ordnance clearance. |
| Recovery potential | Repeated status evidence, matched-season imagery/nightlights, network alternatives | Conditional recovery trajectory and resilience signals | Recovery of illumination is not proof of employment or income recovery. |

The indicator catalogue specifies dataset IDs, extraction method, unit, expected confidence, and substitution limits for each proposed indicator. Confidence shown there is an expectation subject to actual asset review, not a pre-assigned result.

## 5. Thematic Assessment Methods and Reporting

### 5.1 Functionality and accessibility

Classify operating status as documented open, partial, closed, historical only, or unknown. Each status needs an observation date and source. Report accessibility independently as modeled walking and freight travel times under named road/bridge scenarios. A route to a closed market is physical access, not access to functioning trade. Include an all-market network for infrastructure planning and a separate currently documented-operating-market network for usable-service scenarios. Unknown-status markets are excluded from the conservative operating scenario but included in an explicit alternative scenario.

Report commodity availability, price stress, service continuity, and infrastructure constraints separately. The adapted functionality proxy in the prioritization framework is a screening convenience, not a replacement for full multidimensional findings or a cash-transfer feasibility decision.

### 5.2 Infrastructure condition

Digitize market boundaries, buildings, roofed trading space, access points, major loading areas, documented sanitation/water/power assets, and proposed sites separately. With adequate imagery, classify visible features as apparently intact, visible damage, severe visible destruction, possible change, or unobservable. Define damage against observable structures at baseline, not all OSM buildings or a guessed stall count.

Use Sentinel-2 and Landsat for neighborhood-scale land-cover/burn/change screening only. Sentinel-1 backscatter/coherence change is a candidate disturbance signal, not validated building destruction. If very-high-resolution imagery or usable pre-existing damage products are unavailable, suppress structure-level damage percentages and publish area-level uncertainty instead.

Distinguish lack of a roof from nonfunctioning stalls; temporary shade may be difficult to detect. Existing road and building layers indicate mapped assets, not current repair condition. A satellite-observed clear space is not proof of lawful vacant land.

### 5.3 Supply chains, trade routes, and market connectivity

Build directed origin-hub-market links for cereals, vegetables, fruit, livestock, dairy, fish, and essential non-food goods where supported. Candidate contextual corridors to investigate include eastern supply and import links via Port Sudan and inland hubs, agricultural links through Gezira/Gedaref, northern connections, and western livestock connections. These are research hypotheses until documented for the relevant period, not confirmed current flows.

Store commodity, season, origin/destination, source date, route status, quantity if actually reported, and evidence type. Display reported flows as solid directional lines and modeled alternatives as dashed lines. Use equal width when tonnage is unknown; never create weighted Sankey flows from invented volumes. Distinguish exposure along an entire corridor from events near the destination market. The SAMRA report referenced in REF02 must be obtained and reviewed before its substantive corridor claims are adopted.

Assess road-network redundancy through edge-disjoint feasible paths, known bridge dependencies, and detour ratios after selected bridge/road removals. Node centrality is a potential network role, not observed trade. Map upstream and downstream market dependencies so rehabilitation does not displace bottlenecks to adjoining nodes.

### 5.4 Commodity availability, diversity, prices, and affordability

Define the eligible commodity basket by market role before scoring. For general food markets, consider sorghum, wheat/flour, millet where relevant, pulses, edible oil, sugar, salt, vegetables, fruit, dairy, meat/fish, and culturally appropriate nutritious foods, subject to actual monitoring coverage. For specialized markets, use appropriate product/grade/species groups and compare within type.

Extract commodity, unit, grade, retail/wholesale level, market, currency, date, reported availability, and collection method. Convert units only with documented conversion factors. Keep official and parallel exchange-rate series separate; present local-currency prices first. Use constant prices only if a suitable, geographically relevant deflator exists, otherwise explicitly label nominal prices.

Availability coverage = documented available eligible items / eligible items with valid availability observations; also publish observation completeness = observed eligible items / total eligible basket. Missing price rows are not stockouts, and a price-series count is not commodity diversity. Report month-on-month and matched-year changes; calculate volatility from a sufficiently complete same-item series, not a handful of mismatched prices. Correlated prices can suggest linkage but do not prove integration or causal transport costs.

Assess affordability only with a documented income/wage or assistance denominator from a comparable period and population. A minimum basket cost alone indicates expenditure requirements, not household capacity to buy it. If purchasing-power evidence is missing, report that gap rather than equating a large population with effective demand.

### 5.5 Economic activity and business ecosystem

Use dated commercial POIs, industrial/logistics footprints, known warehouses, financial-service points, documented service status, and corrected nightlight trends. Distinguish formal businesses and institutions from informal traders likely underrepresented in mapping. Never use raw OSM edit counts as recovery: they can reflect a mapping campaign.

Nighttime radiance is interpreted at neighborhood or larger scale. A small market occupies less than a useful VIIRS footprint; surrounding residential lights, grid restoration, generators, seasonality, fires, and sensor changes can dominate. Do not publish market-level GDP, trade value, jobs, or vendor counts derived from nightlights. Where the indicator cannot isolate a meaningful signal, mark it unavailable, not zero recovery.

### 5.6 Livelihood opportunities and constraints

| Conditional opportunity | Evidence needed | Constraints and design implications |
|---|---|---|
| Produce retail, aggregation, sorting, storage | Documented production links, demand context, spoilage/handling constraints | No cold-chain investment without reliable power, water, utilization, fee and maintenance model. |
| Livestock/fish services | Verified specialized market role, handling chain, water/sanitation evidence | Veterinary/food safety requirements, drainage, waste treatment, and safe transport. |
| Repair, fabrication, logistics, transport | Documented asset-repair demand, feasible access, procurement pipeline | Avoid assuming training alone creates demand; use market-linked procurement and decent-work safeguards. |
| Safe market cleaning and rehabilitation work | Approved scope, waste plan, safety/ordnance clearances, labor safeguards | No community cash-for-work removal of suspected explosive remnants or hazardous rubble. |
| Women/youth enterprise support | Existing constraints evidence, eligible groups, service-provider capacity | Transparent access, anti-harassment, accessible facilities, childcare feasibility, and non-exclusion of informal vendors. |
| Finance and digital payments | Verified providers, liquidity, agent coverage, consumer protection | Do not recommend lending where debt risk or lack of demand makes grants or services more appropriate. |

Quantify jobs only after applying a documented labor input per unit of approved works, with duration and unique-person versus job-day distinction. Business opportunities here are hypotheses, not beneficiary counts or proven income gains. Women and youth should have equitable access to the whole market, not be relegated to poorly connected designated spaces.

### 5.7 Population density and consumer catchments

Use one selected gridded population baseline and an independent alternative for sensitivity, not their sum. Reconcile locality totals with date-aligned official/UN estimates and DTM current-resident information where compatible. DTM returnees and IDPs are not additive to an existing total unless definitions, geography, residency, and periods permit it. A cumulative return count is not a current total population.

Model low, central, and high current population allocations, preserving each locality's scenario total. Publish theoretical 15/30/60-minute walking or mode-specific catchments, and nearest-market allocated potential residents. Overlapping catchment totals cannot be summed. Population served in dashboard shorthand must be labeled "modeled reachable residents" with date/scenario, not actual customers or unique program beneficiaries.

Physical reach is only one dimension of access. Cost, disability, gendered mobility, insecurity, opening times, checkpoints, and discrimination may make modeled access unattainable. Report these as documentary constraints or unknowns, not eliminated barriers.

### 5.8 Security and conflict disruption

Analyze appropriate violent-event categories over 90/180/365 days, event-location precision, and reported transport/market disruptions. Use event-level IDs to deduplicate; review broad-place geocoding before small buffers. Compare multiple distances such as 1/5/10 km as sensitivity scenarios, not safety zones. Reporting intensity and delayed updates affect counts; do not compare raw reports as if surveillance completeness were uniform.

Use recent exposure as a negatively directed investment-readiness indicator. Keep humanitarian need visible separately so highly affected populations are not erased by lower feasibility. Do not publish sensitive checkpoints, individual movement patterns, precise security deployments, or actionable unverified safe-route recommendations. Security and explosive-ordnance reviews are independent investment gates, not satisfied by low ACLED counts.

### 5.9 Flood, climate, water, and environmental risks

Intersect market footprints, access roads, catchments, and supply nodes with JRC river-flood hazard scenarios and dated observed flood extents where available. A 100-year river-flood map represents approximately 1% annual exceedance under model assumptions, not a once-per-century schedule, and may not capture pluvial flooding, blocked drains, embankment failures, or altered urban terrain. An observed flood map is one event, not return-period risk.

CHIRPS precipitation anomalies describe regional rainfall context, not market-plot drainage. Analyze local and upstream basin rainfall separately for Nile-related risk. Use Sentinel/Landsat NDVI to examine producer-area vegetation, not consumer welfare or trader income. Use Landsat surface temperature for hot-surface exposure and ERA5-Land for broader air-temperature context; neither alone gives occupational heat stress or WBGT. Shade, ventilation, working hours, water, humidity, and labor protection require design review.

Use surface-water occurrence plus documented water-supply status to screen water constraints; surface water is not potable water or evidence that a borehole works. Include solid waste, wastewater, hazardous debris, fire access, fuel handling, disease controls, and cumulative water-use implications in intervention screening. Do not propose groundwater pumping or solar irrigation without abstraction and sustainability review.

### 5.10 Resilience and recovery potential

Track documented reopening/status, matched-season physical change, applicable nightlight signals, alternative routes, supplier-source diversity when documented, and restoration of supporting services. Recovery requires repeated observations. Where an event date and observation sequence exist, bracket recovery between the last non-recovered and first recovered observation; do not infer exact recovery days between sparse images.

Evaluate at least: reference network, wet-season disruption, selected bridge closure, renewed insecurity, and low-return/population scenarios. Report who loses access and which investments retain value across scenarios. Favor modular, maintainable assets and enabling services where uncertainty is high, while keeping future high-need investments visible for preparation.

## 7. Prioritization and Investment Logic

Use the requested six-criterion weights: potential population served 20%, accessibility 15%, adapted functionality 20%, lower recorded conflict exposure 15%, economic significance 15%, and rehabilitation feasibility 15%. All scores run 0-100 with higher values meaning stronger conditional readiness/opportunity. Exact transforms, missing-data handling, equations, safeguards, scenario tests, and an explicitly synthetic calculation are specified in the prioritization framework.

Need is a separate axis: documented deficits, population exclusion, vulnerability evidence, and severity of damage. Do not invert the full score or call the highest-functioning market the highest-need site. Feasibility gates and evidence completeness precede ranks. A real ranking is not produced until data meet those rules.

Maintain three portfolios: ready for preparation subject to current safeguards; high need requiring enabling work or additional documentation; and unknown requiring evidence resolution. Unknown is not a low-priority judgment.

## 8. Rehabilitation Packages

Timelines below are planning horizons from a decision to proceed, not promises of delivery or permission to enter a site.

| Horizon | Conditional package | Minimum evidence and safeguards | Monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick enabling measures, 0-6 months | Cleared safe waste removal; repair of documented minor WASH/lighting/access deficits; non-exclusive temporary shade/loading organization | Safety and ordnance clearance, disposal plan, lawful permission, accessibility review, vendor non-displacement safeguards, maintenance owner | Verified service restoration, unobstructed entrances, dated cleared works records; no assumed jobs multiplier. |
| Medium term, 6-18 months | Structurally assessed shelter rehabilitation, drainage and sanitation, safe loading/storage, reliable small-scale power where appropriate | Engineering and drainage design, tenure/permits, costed options, demand and utilization evidence, procurement, environmental/social and fire controls | Completed and usable assets, service reliability, occupancy only if existing administrative records can verify it. |
| Long term, 18-36+ months | Wholesale/logistics links, resilient cold chain where justified, regional producer-market integration, finance ecosystem support | Corridor and utilities feasibility, business/O&M model, inclusion, sustainable water/energy, land and conflict sensitivity, approved financing | Access resilience, documented service continuity, repeat market monitoring; separate outputs from livelihood outcomes. |

For each market prepare a one-page investment concept: documented problem; beneficiaries as a range and definition; options including no-build/service alternatives; proposed package; dependencies; expected results; costs source and price date; uncertainty/contingency; safeguards; responsible owner; operating-cost arrangement; and go/no-go evidence gaps. Do not use the reference's planned shelter capacities as validated bills of quantities.

## 10. Monitoring, Refresh, and Reporting Controls

- Security and route context: review weekly and after major disruptions, explicitly non-operational and source-dated.
- Prices, availability, and documented status: monthly where observations exist.
- Population/displacement: each compatible DTM/UN release; never automatically add totals to a baseline.
- Imagery/activity: monthly or quarterly according to coverage, quality, and product availability.
- Hazard layers: annual/version updates plus major flood events; separate static susceptibility from observed events.
- MCDA: quarterly or material-change trigger; keep anchors fixed within a version, archive previous rankings, and explain rank shifts.

Outcome framework: restored usable trading infrastructure and services; improved potential physical access; documented continuity of essential goods; and equitable recovery opportunities. Publish outputs that are measurable from the approved secondary sources. Actual income, employment, satisfaction, safe access, and trader inclusion outcomes remain unverified unless independently collected existing records can support them. Do not claim attribution to UNDP from a before/after satellite comparison.

## 11. Report Assembly and Donor Acceptance
