# ArcGIS StoryMap Storyboard and Analytical Products

Authoring blueprint, not a published ArcGIS item | Khartoum State | 27 October 2026

## 1. Editorial Direction

**Title:** Khartoum State Markets

**Subtitle:** Secondary evidence for market rehabilitation and livelihood recovery

**Central narrative:** Restoring markets requires more than rebuilding structures. Recovery depends on connected suppliers, reachable trading spaces, reliable services, viable businesses, and safeguards that protect people and livelihoods.

**Audience:** UNDP program teams, state/local partners, humanitarian market and cash specialists, donors, and recovery planners. Maintain a public narrative and a restricted evidence/application workspace; neither should imply operational safe-route guidance.

Adapt the reference's geographic sequence: context, population and supply connections, market profiles, site-specific needs, integrated recovery. Extend it to state-wide systems, accessibility, risk, and transparent prioritization. The reference is a PDF of an existing StoryMap, not an exportable template. Reuse its storytelling logic, not its text, images, live edit URL, or restricted services. Its `NOT YET CLASSIFIED` content needs owner clearance.

### Visual system

- Predominantly light paper/white background, charcoal text, restrained UNDP-brand-compatible blue for focus, teal for documented operation, amber for constraints, rust/red for recorded exposure, and neutral gray hatching for unknowns. Follow the actual organizational brand guide when available; these are design suggestions, not official color codes.
- Illustrative accessible palette: background `#FFFFFF`, text `#20262C`, focus `#006EB6`, activity `#007F75`, constraint `#9C5B00`, exposure `#A63434`, unknown `#666666`. Verify contrast in actual component combinations. Never rely on color alone.
- Use a supported StoryMaps theme, legible headings and body text, bilingual names with correct Arabic rendering, compact captions, generous map widths, and consistent map extents. Prefer repeated sidecar profile panels over a long sequence of unrelated charts.
- Lead with a cleared, real Khartoum market image or dated satellite overview showing the study area, not stock disaster imagery or a generated documentary scene. Obtain permission and caption date/location. If no reusable photograph is available, use an appropriately licensed satellite or cartographic cover.
- One clear analytical message per map/frame. No 3D extrusion for population counts, decorative flows, false precision, or categorical colors on incomparable scores.
- Show observation date, source ID/link, evidence type, geographic support, and confidence in map captions/popups. Use "Not assessed" or "Unknown" for missing values, not a blank graphic or zero.
- Use publicly cleared content only. Existing photographs must retain provenance and permissions; do not identify vulnerable individuals. Never use generated people/photos as evidence.

### Reader path

Cover; nine sections; short methods/source appendix; credits and release date. A concise donor reader can follow the narrative in about 10-15 minutes; the Experience Builder evidence workspace supports deeper comparison. Limit heavy embeds; provide a static cleared figure and text summary when an embed fails or the connection is slow.

The prose below is safe draft framing. Numerical/site-specific findings must be inserted only after analysis and review. Do not publish unresolved authoring notes, speculative rankings, empty chart axes, or synthetic example scores as findings.

## Section 1: Introduction

**Question:** Why assess markets now, and what can this assessment establish?

**Draft narrative:**

> Markets connect food supply, work, services, and daily life. In Khartoum State, rehabilitation decisions must consider not only damaged trading spaces but also the systems that allow goods, people, and payments to reach them. This assessment brings together existing reports, open geospatial data, and dated satellite evidence to identify recovery opportunities and the conditions needed for responsible investment.

> No new field survey was conducted. Physical change and modeled accessibility are assessed separately from reported business operation. Where evidence cannot establish current conditions, the assessment states the gap rather than replacing it with an assumed finding.

**Blocks:** cover image and title; short introduction; full-width locator map M01 from Sudan to Khartoum State; scope/limitations paragraph; compact method diagram from secondary evidence to analysis to conditional investment.

**Evidence:** REF01-03; S01; verified market register. Show "14 provisional source entries; 15 intended in concept note; final scope unresolved" only in a clearly labeled draft/inception product. In a final release show actual confirmed counts and exclusions, not the intended sample as completed coverage.

**Map behavior:** overview bookmark then locality view; verified sites only, with unresolved records listed outside the map. Scale and boundaries are illustrative, not official endorsement.

**Transition:** From individual locations to the conflict-affected systems in which markets operate.

## Section 2: Context Analysis

**Question:** How have conflict, movement, services, and purchasing power changed the recovery setting?

**Draft narrative:**

> Conflict and population movement have changed both supply and demand. People returning to a neighborhood do not necessarily return with purchasing power, functioning utilities, or a viable business. National economic stress provides important context, but it does not describe the condition of every market in Khartoum State.

**Blocks:** three-frame sidecar: conflict/disruption timeline, population movement and scenario map M03, economic and humanitarian context. Use small-multiple dates rather than an animation that implies complete continuous observations.

**Evidence:** DTM dated observations and definitions; OCHA applicable reports; FEWS NET/Sudan market context; WFP economic context; appropriate price series. Use S42's September 2026 analysis as national/regional context and identify projection periods separately from observations. S43 explains external fuel/trade transmission but is not a measured Khartoum route inventory. Do not reuse national headline totals as local estimates.

**Metrics:** dated population scenario ranges; data coverage; documented service interruptions; comparable nominal price change only where series exist. Do not combine source totals with different denominators or refer to returnees as all current consumers.

**Fallback:** if current harmonized population totals are unavailable, show a dated baseline map and an explicit scenario explanation, not a falsely updated density surface.

## Section 3: Market System Overview

**Question:** What roles do the markets play, and how are supplies connected?

**Draft narrative:**

> A neighborhood food market, a wholesale hub, and a specialized livestock or fish market serve different functions. Rehabilitation should preserve these connections rather than treat every market as an identical collection of buildings. Reported supply links and modeled transport alternatives are therefore shown separately.

**Blocks:** market hierarchy M01; directed commodity-network map M02; compact commodity/season matrix; short profile sequence grouped by locality and market type.

**Evidence:** verified market roles, FEWS NET trade maps, permitted logistics reporting, and SAMRA only after the actual report is obtained and reviewed. Give REF02 a clear attribution when describing its historical three-site lessons.

**Interaction:** commodity and season filters in the evidence application; narrative map uses a few fixed, understandable views. Solid reported links, dashed modeled routes, equal widths when quantity is unknown. Use a schematic node-link view instead of a quantity-based Sankey if volumes are not supported.

**Profile framing:** retain Hilat Kuku, Soug 6, and Umdawan Ban as illustrative reference cases only until current evidence is available. Label existing, east/west sub-sites, and proposed parcels distinctly. Administrative names must be verified before publication.

## Section 4: Market Accessibility

**Question:** Which populations can physically reach a market under the modeled conditions, and where do barriers persist?

**Draft narrative:**

> A nearby market is not always reachable. Rivers, bridge dependence, disconnected roads, damaged infrastructure, travel costs, and protection concerns may separate people from trading spaces. Network models show potential physical reach under explicit assumptions; they do not prove that a journey is safe, affordable, or possible for everyone.

**Blocks:** network/service-area map M04; scenario catchments M03; influence allocation M05; baseline versus selected-bridge-closure sidecar; short access dashboard D03 and unique population dashboard D02, preferably linked to one lightweight embedded application rather than several heavy iframes.

**Indicators:** 15/30/60-minute walking reach; local weighted median minutes; disconnected share; unique reachable resident ranges; freight detour ratio. Keep walking/freight and population scenarios separate. Explain slower walking as a sensitivity case, not a verified disability-access assessment.

**Captions:** potential reachable residents, not footfall/customers. Market catchment totals overlap unless explicitly unique-allocated. Influence zones are nearest-time allocations, not measured consumer preference. Do not use a calibrated-looking Huff model without behavior data and a transparent alternative-assumptions statement.

**Fallback:** where site location or road topology is unresolved, show no service area and state why; do not manufacture a circle and call it a travel-time catchment.

## Section 5: Market Functionality Assessment

**Question:** What is known about physical condition, operation, commodities, and supporting services?

**Draft narrative:**

> Buildings and trading activity are not the same. A roof may remain while a market lacks suppliers, working capital, sanitation, or customers. This assessment keeps visible physical condition, documented operating status, commodity availability, and service continuity distinct, and combines them only where the evidence supports an adapted screening score.

**Blocks:** repeated market sidecars; market-profile dashboard D01; physical-condition map M06; correctly dated before/after imagery; comparable commodity/price chart; neighborhood recovery signals M11 and D05 where valid.

**Repeated profile template:** name and verified locality; role; observation dates; catchment/access range; visible condition and observable denominator; documented status and commodity coverage; services/finance; key constraints; confidence/gaps; three conditional actions. Use the same structure for all sites, including unknowns.

**Imagery swipe:** only if matched images have adequate resolution, registration, dates, scale, reuse rights, and explanatory captions. Do not use Sentinel-2 to imply stall-level damage; use a neighborhood-change panel or static figure when more appropriate. A swipe is a storytelling device, not proof of cause.

**Reference lesson:** REF02's reported Soug 6 siting/utilization problem illustrates why covered space alone is not a demand measure. The reported percentages and planned capacities must remain attributed historical claims unless corroborated with current evidence.

**Unknowns:** actual trader numbers, turnover, inventory days, finance access, participation shares, and food quality stay unknown unless suitable existing records support them.

## Section 6: Vulnerability and Risk Analysis

**Question:** Which disruptions and hazards could undermine recovery, and what must be resolved before works?

**Draft narrative:**

> Recovery opportunities remain exposed to conflict, disrupted supply routes, flood and drainage problems, heat, water constraints, and unsafe debris. Recorded events and hazard models help screen these pressures, but cannot certify safe access or site suitability. Investment preparation must retain separate security, explosive-ordnance, land, engineering, and environmental safeguards.

**Blocks:** generalized conflict exposure M07; river/observed flood maps M08; climate/heat/water context M10; safeguards and missing-evidence strip; D04 conflict summary for internal use or a cleared public aggregation.

**Evidence:** precision-reviewed event dataset and UN reports; global JRC flood scenarios; observed flood extents; CHIRPS, surface-temperature, water/service context. Keep hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and confirmed damage distinct.

**Design:** public map generalizes sensitive event and corridor detail. No checkpoint map, live safe-route guidance, or assertion that a zero-event area is safe. Unknown gate status is visible and blocks an investment-ready label.

## Section 7: Market Prioritization

**Question:** How are candidate opportunities compared fairly, transparently, and under uncertainty?

**Draft narrative:**

> The screening model combines potential population reach, access, functionality, recorded conflict exposure, economic-system role, and rehabilitation feasibility. Its weights are explicit. The resulting score describes conditional opportunity and readiness, not humanitarian need. Markets with severe needs but unresolved conditions remain visible in a separate preparation and enabling-work portfolio.

**Blocks:** six-weight infographic I04; conditional priority map M12; contribution/ranking matrix I02; interval chart; need-readiness quadrant; link to full methods and source catalogue.

**Weights:** population 20%; accessibility 15%; functionality 20%; lower recorded conflict exposure 15%; economic significance 15%; feasibility 15%.

**Disclosure:** show lower/upper score, evidence coverage, confidence mix, gate status, and scenario stability. No rank for unverified geometry, insufficient evidence, or unresolved mandatory gates. No synthetic examples in public results. Scores alone do not set rehabilitation need or funding awards.

**Interaction:** show precomputed approved scenarios. A separate analyst tool may explore weights, but the published base weights stay fixed and distinguishable. Use priority bands or ties when ranks are unstable.

**Fallback:** if no site meets eligibility, publish a gap/preparation map and the validated method, not an invented ranking.

## Section 8: Rehabilitation Opportunities

**Question:** Which packages address evidenced constraints, and what dependencies determine timing?

**Draft narrative:**

> Recovery packages should address the constraint that prevents useful trading, not simply the most visible damaged structure. In some locations the first requirement may be safe access, land resolution, sanitation, maintenance arrangements, or a viable site layout. In others, qualified rehabilitation of existing assets may offer greater value than new construction.

**Blocks:** phased intervention matrix; market-to-package table/map M13; livelihood opportunity infographic I03; two or three supported investment concepts including no-build alternatives. Show enabling conditions before construction proposals.

**Quick measures:** only cleared, lawful, safe waste/service/access and minor repair actions with responsible maintenance. No unqualified community removal of suspected explosive remnants.

**Medium term:** designed shelter/WASH/drainage/storage improvements after tenure, engineering, demand, inclusion, cost, and safeguards review.

**Long term:** regional logistics and producer-market linkages, cold chain where viable, sustainable energy/finance systems, and water-conscious agricultural integration. Do not recommend solar irrigation solely because it appears in the benchmark; assess abstraction and market demand.

**Inclusion:** accessible circulation, safe facilities, equitable trading-space access, fair allocations, anti-harassment mechanisms, and non-displacement safeguards. Do not presume a segregated women's area is sufficient or preferable without applicable evidence.

**Results:** distinguish proposed capacities, completed assets, unique people, job-days, and livelihood outcomes. No unsupported employment or income multipliers.

## Section 9: Conclusions and Recommendations

**Question:** What can decision-makers act on now, and what remains conditional?

**Draft narrative:**

> The strongest investment case combines an evidenced recovery need with a useful market-system role, potential access for residents, a viable operating model, and resolved safeguards. Secondary evidence can support transparent screening and options appraisal. It cannot remove the need for any legally or technically required safety, tenure, engineering, and protection checks before implementation.

**Blocks:** three decision messages; confirmed shortlist only if supported; high-need/enabling-work portfolio; evidence gaps and owners; refresh schedule; methods/data links; credits and classification statement.

**Immediate actions:** resolve market identities and scope; obtain analysis-ready data and permissions; run and review the GIS/evidence pipeline; approve scoring assumptions; prepare conditional intervention options. Report current findings only after those steps.

**Final footnote:** methods version, analysis cutoff, heterogeneous source dates, no new field data, model limitations, rights, boundary disclaimer, and contact role rather than unnecessary personal details.

Design five thematic views. They may be separate ArcGIS Dashboards or coordinated pages in one Experience Builder application. Pre-aggregate at valid keys and prevent joins from multiplying populations or event counts. Each view exposes current filters, source dates, and an evidence-completeness measure.

| ID | View and widgets | Filters/actions | Correct aggregation and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|

| D02 | Population served: low/central/high reachable residents; unallocated population; unique/overlap comparison; demographic context where valid | One population/network scenario at a time; geography and market selection | Sum unique allocation or union reach only; no addition across overlapping markets/scenarios/periods; label potential residents. |

**Standard popup:** name Arabic/English; market role; locality; observed period; evidence type and confidence; potential residents by scenario; travel-time assumptions; documented status; key gap; intervention condition; source link and methods version. Hide sensitive details by removing them from public data, not merely popup configuration.

**Empty state:** "Not assessed for this market/period" with reason and latest historical date if available. Do not show a green status from the absence of a conflict record or a blank bar at zero.

## 5. ArcGIS Authoring Sequence

1. Complete local GIS/evidence QA, scoring, independent desk review, and content clearance before producing result maps. A methodology-only StoryMap can be published earlier only if explicitly labeled and cleared.
2. Publish sanitized hosted layers/views and approved imagery derivatives. Set appropriate ownership, groups, sharing, and credits; test records and fields via anonymous REST access.
3. Configure and save thematic web maps M01-M13 with consistent IDs/aliases, bookmarks, scales, popups, legends, and missing-data symbology. Not all need to be live embeds; use static maps for slow or complex layers.
4. Build D01-D05 views and Experience Builder navigation: Market Directory, Access and Catchments, Risk and Recovery, Comparison, Evidence and Methods. Precompute official scenarios; test filtering and aggregation before embedding.
5. In StoryMaps create the title/cover, nine navigation headings, one or two sidecars for geographic progression, repeated profile panels, matched imagery swipe where valid, and concise decision tables. Prefer fewer useful embeds over many competing dashboards.
6. Link profile panels to supported market selections in the application and verify deep links. Provide printable sources/methods and accessible text/static alternatives.
7. Review English and Arabic name display; have the full translation professionally checked before public Arabic release. A separate Arabic story or synchronized companion may be more robust than mixed-direction paragraphs; test the chosen organizational theme.
8. Test desktop and 360 px mobile, keyboard navigation, contrast, legend readability, popup text, map scroll behavior, embed sign-in state, low-bandwidth fallbacks, and screen-reader summaries.
9. Run a public-versus-restricted dependency audit: stories, web maps, layers, imagery, dashboards, app, downloads, photographs, and service fields. Owner login success is not a publication test.
10. Obtain program/editorial/GIS/data-protection clearance; publish the release with analysis date/method version; archive the item manifest; schedule refresh and reassess after material conflict, movement, flood, or service changes.

## 6. Asset Manifest and Acceptance

The production manifest must list `asset_id`, title, purpose, ArcGIS item ID or approved static file, owner, source IDs, observation period, method version, public/restricted classification, license, dependencies, refresh date, and approval status. No item IDs or live URLs are fabricated in this blueprint.

Release only when every map's numeric claim has a valid source/model, every reference image has rights and dates, all filters preserve denominators, missing evidence is visible, the six weights match the approved version, and sensitive information is removed from public services. If analysis cannot produce supported results, publish an evidence-gap narrative with next actions rather than a visually complete but unsupported assessment.
