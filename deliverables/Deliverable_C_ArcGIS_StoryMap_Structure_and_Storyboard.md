# Khartoum Markets: Complete ArcGIS StoryMap Structure and Storyboard

## Authoring Basis

This document specifies Deliverable C only: the complete narrative and authoring structure for an ArcGIS StoryMap on selected markets in Khartoum State. It does not constitute a published story, a completed market assessment, or an instruction to populate missing results with assumed values. The GIS methodology, market register, and scoring configuration remain unchanged.

The storyboard follows the agreed nine-section progression from Introduction to Conclusions and Recommendations. Within every section, the nine requested components are specified separately: objectives, narrative, maps, images, charts, GIS analysis, key messages, interactions, and layouts. Reader-facing narrative is provided as original draft copy. Tables, media specifications, scene directions, and evidence gates are production instructions and should not be pasted into the public narrative.

**Working title:** Khartoum State Markets

**Subtitle:** Evidence for rehabilitation and livelihood recovery

**Narrative proposition:** A useful market is a connection between people, goods, services, and opportunity. Rehabilitation decisions should restore those connections, not merely replace structures.

**Primary audience:** UNDP program and technical teams, recovery partners, humanitarian market specialists, public authorities, and donors. The public story should explain the evidence and decisions without exposing restricted operational information. A restricted evidence application may accompany it where access and licensing permit.

**Evidence status:** The supplied reference documents have been reviewed, but verified market locations, current analytical layers, actual market scores, and ArcGIS item IDs are not supplied by this storyboard. Its narrative therefore contains no invented site counts, population totals, damage rates, rankings, investment costs, or beneficiary claims.

## Shared Design and Evidence Rules

### Visual Direction

Use a supported StoryMaps theme consistent with the actual organizational brand guide. Favor a predominantly light background, readable charcoal text, restrained blue for orientation, teal for supported operation, amber for constraints, rust/red for exposure, and gray/hatching for unknowns. These are semantic design recommendations, not official UNDP brand specifications.

Keep geography legible and avoid decorative 3D extrusion, animated flows without measured quantities, complete-looking radar charts with missing inputs, and oversized headline statistics detached from dates or denominators. A photograph or dated satellite overview should reveal the actual subject, not merely provide atmosphere.

Use verified Arabic and English market names consistently. Original source spelling remains in the evidence record; public labels should use an approved name/alias crosswalk. Do not call all listed sites Bahri or Sharg El Neel without resolving the administrative discrepancy. The intended sample of 15 and the 14 identifiable source entries must not appear as completed coverage of 15 assessed sites.

### Reader-Facing Evidence Language

| Evidence situation | Preferred language | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Existing dated report | "The cited assessment reported ... in [actual observation period]." | "We observed ..." when no new observation occurred. |
| Suitable imagery | "The dated imagery indicates visible ... within the interpretable area." | "The market is structurally safe" or "all stalls are damaged." |
| Access/population model | "Potential reachable residents under the stated scenario." | "Customers served," "daily footfall," or "beneficiaries" without evidence. |
| Event dataset | "Qualifying recorded events in the selected window." | "Safe area" or "no conflict" from zero records. |
| Incomplete evidence | "Current status could not be established from the available sources." | An empty graphic implying zero or a low-performance score. |
| Proposed intervention | "Candidate option, subject to the stated conditions." | "Approved project" or "jobs created" for a proposal. |

Every quantitative caption/popup must identify the unit, geographic support, observation/model period, scenario if relevant, source, coverage/uncertainty, and interpretation limit. Source dates will not be replaced by the story publication date.

### Interaction and Platform Boundaries

Use StoryMaps for a guided reading experience: text, web maps, sidecars, cleared images, appropriate image galleries/swipes, charts as approved figures or supported embeds, and links to deeper evidence. Check the actual organizational version and supported block/layout options before authoring.

Do not assume StoryMaps itself provides arbitrary cross-widget filtering, live MCDA recalculation, synchronized selections across independent embeds, or automatic cross-app state persistence. Such functions belong in a tested Dashboard or Experience Builder companion where supported. The default should be precomputed approved scenarios, not a bespoke scoring application.

Map actions, sidecar state changes, or bookmarks should be configured only where supported and tested in the selected layout. Narrative statements may explain analytical limitations, but no reader-facing copy should instruct people to click, scroll, hover, use a shortcut, or discover a hidden control. Use conventional control labels and tooltips in the application instead.

For mobile and low-bandwidth readers, provide a static cleared figure and an accessible textual conclusion for each essential visualization. A blocked embed, missing sign-in, or unreadable mobile chart must not remove the story's substantive finding.

## Section 1: Introduction

### 1.1 Objectives

- Establish Khartoum State and selected market sites as the geographic focus.
- Explain why market rehabilitation is a livelihoods and market-system issue, not solely a construction issue.
- State the secondary-only assessment approach and define its decision-support purpose.
- Clarify selected-site coverage and the difference between confirmed sites and unresolved registry entries.

### 1.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Khartoum's markets and recovery**

Markets are part of the infrastructure of everyday life. They bring essential goods closer to households, create outlets for producers, and support trading, transport, handling, repair, and other services. Their usefulness depends on more than the condition of a building: people and supplies must be able to reach them, basic services must function, and businesses must have a reason and a means to trade.

This assessment examines selected markets in Khartoum State to inform rehabilitation and livelihood-recovery decisions. It considers market sites alongside their surrounding populations, transport connections, supply networks, services, and operating conditions. The aim is to identify where investment could address a documented constraint and what conditions must be resolved before action.

The assessment uses existing reports, published datasets, mapped infrastructure, and dated satellite evidence. No new field survey was conducted. Documentary findings, visible physical changes, and modelled estimates are kept distinct. Where the available evidence cannot establish current operation or another essential condition, that uncertainty remains part of the conclusion.

The story begins with the recovery setting, then follows goods and people toward the markets before examining site conditions, risks, and possible responses.

**Transition:** The condition of a market must be understood within the changing conditions around it.

Market geolocation/identity resolution, spatial join to adopted localities, market-type classification from evidence, and scope coverage. No travel-time, population, or score result is required in this opening section.

### 1.7 Key Messages

1. Rehabilitation should restore useful trading connections as well as assets.
2. The assessment covers a defined selected cohort, not an assumed census of every major market.
3. Secondary evidence supports transparent screening but does not recreate unobserved field findings.

Use a locator-to-locality change of view, readable verified-site popups, and approved market-type visibility where supported. Popups should initially show name, locality, role, evidence period, and identity confidence rather than a dense table of unresolved indicators.

## Section 2: Context Analysis

### 2.1 Objectives

- Explain how conflict, displacement/return, services, and economic conditions influence market recovery.
- Distinguish national/regional context from evidence applicable to a locality or site.
- Establish the temporal comparison used later in the story.
- Show why potential population presence and purchasing power are separate questions.

### 2.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: A changing recovery context**

The conflict that began in April 2023 disrupted the conditions on which market exchange depends. The assessment considers damage and service interruptions alongside population movement, supply constraints, and pressures on household and business resources. These changes do not occur at the same pace or with the same intensity in every locality.

Displacement and return alter where people live and where demand may emerge. A recorded movement or cumulative return figure is not, however, a count of all residents currently present. Nor does return alone establish that households have recovered income, utilities, secure access, or a functioning business. Population scenarios therefore provide a conditional view of potential market reach rather than a claim about customers or purchasing power.

The economic context also matters. Higher prices, currency changes, transport costs, and limited liquidity can affect the ability to buy and replenish goods. Their relevance to an individual market depends on the geography and dates of the evidence. National trends are presented as context, while market-level conclusions require applicable observations.

The following comparisons preserve observation dates and separate documented conditions from modelled scenarios. They establish the setting for recovery decisions, not a uniform account of conditions throughout Khartoum State.

**Transition:** Within this setting, different markets perform different roles in moving goods and supporting livelihoods.

1. Recovery conditions are uneven and time-specific.

## Section 3: Market System Overview

### 3.1 Objectives

- Describe the roles of neighborhood, mixed, wholesale, and specialized markets using evidence.
- Show documented commodity connections, intermediate hubs, and relevant supplier areas.
- Explain dependence on transport, logistics, services, and institutions beyond the market boundary.
- Introduce the market-profile structure without pre-judging rehabilitation priorities.

### 3.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: The market system**

A market's role is defined by the exchanges and connections it supports. Some trading spaces primarily serve nearby households; others aggregate production, support wholesale distribution, or specialize in goods such as livestock or fish. These functions require different facilities and should not be judged against an identical commodity basket or construction template.

The assessment traces reported links between origins, intermediate hubs, and destination markets. It also examines the roads and crossings on which those links may depend. Documentary supply connections and modelled route alternatives remain separate: a feasible line through the road network does not establish that goods currently move along it, in what quantities, or under what conditions.

Supporting services form part of this system. Loading space, storage, water, energy, finance, and market management can determine whether a functioning connection translates into useful trade. Their mapped presence is considered alongside existing evidence of operation and the constraints that businesses face.

This system view helps identify the level at which a problem should be addressed. A site-level repair may be valuable, but a supply interruption, service deficit, or poorly connected trading location may require a complementary response beyond the building itself.

**Transition:** The next question is whether people and supplies can physically reach these trading spaces under the modelled conditions.

Physical centrality may be shown as potential network position but not converted into sales or trade shares.

### 3.7 Key Messages

1. Markets have different roles and require role-appropriate comparisons.
2. Reported trade connections and modelled transport alternatives are different forms of evidence.
3. A constraint outside the market boundary can undermine an otherwise reasonable site intervention.

## Section 4: Market Accessibility

### 4.1 Objectives

- Explain modelled physical access for consumers and separately for freight.
- Show potential resident catchments, overlap, and unique allocation without claiming actual customers.
- Identify disconnection, bridge dependence, and sensitivity to population/network assumptions.
- Communicate the access model's limits, including affordability, protection, disability, and unknown road status.

### 4.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Reaching the market**

Distance on a map is only one part of access. A river, an unavailable crossing, a missing road connection, or an unsuitable entrance can make a nearby trading space difficult to reach. Conversely, a connected market farther away may be more physically accessible within the modelled network.

The analysis estimates travel from representative population origins to verified market entrances. Walking and freight are treated separately, and the calculations include explicit assumptions about speed, restrictions, and connections between origins, roads, and entrances. The resulting travel times describe conditional physical reach, not observed journeys.

Catchment results identify potential residents within selected travel-time thresholds. Individual market catchments may overlap; their totals are not added as if each resident appears only once. A separate allocation assigns each origin to the nearest eligible market under the stated assumptions. That allocation is a planning model, not a record of where people prefer or can afford to shop.

Comparison scenarios examine how access changes with slower travel or a specified crossing disruption. They reveal dependencies that may matter to rehabilitation, while leaving social access, fares, safety, and other barriers visible as documentary findings or unresolved questions.

**Transition:** Physical reach matters, but it does not establish whether the destination provides useful, functioning trade.

| Map/view | Contents | Display and evidence rule |
|---|---|---|

| M03-C: Potential residents | Population scenario and origin-based reachable/unallocated categories. | Distinct unique/overlapping measures; source period and population basis stated. |
| M05-A: Modelled influence | Nearest-network-time origin allocation among the eligible set. | Distinct unallocated class; not measured consumer preference. |
| M04-B: Disruption comparison | Reference versus selected bridge/road restriction or slower-speed case. | Scenario, not a forecast or live access advisory. |
| M04-C: Freight connection, optional | Documented hubs to market entrances with actual modelled route geometry. | Separate mode/speed/period; not OD straight display lines. |

Use sequential access bands, neutral areas for unreachable/unresolved, and a clear distinction between modelled disconnection and data failure. A market with unresolved geometry should have no service-area result.

### 4.4 Images Required

**IMG07: Verified access context.** Existing cleared imagery of a documented market entrance, local access arrangement, or crossing, with its date and limitation.

**IMG08: Network/satellite annotation, conditional.** A reviewed view showing why a route depends on a crossing or barrier. The annotation must explain geometry/topology, not certify passability or expose sensitive operational detail.

A pair of modelled maps is usually more appropriate than unrelated road photographs for the scenario comparison. No photograph should imply a current road condition unless its date and relevance support that claim.

### 4.5 Charts Required

- **CH09: Unique potential resident reach.** Low/central/high population-scenario ranges for the same network/mode and eligible market set. Do not sum alternative scenarios.
- **CH10: Access distribution.** Population by mutually exclusive time bands and unreachable/data-unresolved categories, using a defined origin population. Do not stack cumulative 15-, 30-, and 60-minute totals.
- **CH11: Scenario change.** Paired values or intervals for unique reach, disconnection, or valid freight detour between reference and one named disruption. Disconnected routes are categorical, not finite fake detour values.

If local travel-time medians are shown, state that they are population-weighted among reachable origins and display the disconnected share. Do not obtain a state median by averaging market medians.

### 4.6 GIS Analysis Displayed

Reviewed road topology and mode restrictions; verified entrance/origin connectors; OD-A threshold catchments and OD-B unrestricted local reachability; population-conserving scenarios; unique allocation; service-area envelopes; and selected network disruptions. Connector costs must be charged exactly once, and solver omissions must not silently become zero time or disconnection.

### 4.7 Key Messages

1. Modelled access depends on network structure, entrances, assumptions, and the eligible market set.
2. Potential reachable residents are not actual customers or verified beneficiaries.
3. Rehabilitation can be undermined by a shared crossing or corridor dependency even when a site itself is intact.

### 4.8 Interactive Elements

Use a limited set of approved population, mode, speed, and network scenarios in the access companion, with the current selection visible. D02 and D03 are alternative views of a coordinated application where feasible, not necessarily separate heavy embeds.

A narrative sidecar may compare reference/slower/disruption states. An image/map swipe can be used only when both sides use comparable extent, units, and clearly named scenarios; a wipe should not imply that a hypothetical closure has occurred. The story must retain a static summary of the important difference.

Use a docked sidecar with three map states and one compact chart/companion embed after the explanation. Keep legends and assumptions visible without covering the market or river crossing of interest.

## Section 5: Market Functionality Assessment

### 5.1 Objectives

- Separate visible physical condition from documented operation, commodities, services, finance, and business constraints.
- Present repeatable market profiles with dates, evidence coverage, and uncertainty.
- Use imagery and existing assessments without implying that this study conducted field visits.
- Explain the conditions under which an adapted functionality proxy is eligible.

### 5.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Beyond the buildings**

A standing shelter is not proof of a functioning market, and visible damage does not describe every constraint to recovery. Trade may depend on the condition of access and loading areas, reliable water and sanitation, power, storage, suppliers, finance, and customers' ability to purchase goods. These conditions require different forms of evidence.

Each market profile therefore separates what existing records report about operation from what suitable imagery can show about physical condition. Commodity availability and service status are presented for their actual observation periods. Where a category has not been observed adequately, it remains unknown rather than being assigned a favorable or unfavorable result.

Comparable imagery can help identify visible physical change, but its usefulness depends on resolution, registration, viewing conditions, and how much of the baseline can be interpreted. The share of structures that are observable accompanies any damage fraction. Neighborhood lighting is treated as a broader contextual signal, not a measure of a market's sales or jobs.

Together, these profiles support a more specific diagnosis: whether the evidence points toward repair, service restoration, access/layout improvement, complementary business support, or unresolved questions that prevent a reliable recommendation.

**Transition:** The diagnosis must also account for the pressures that could damage assets or interrupt trading again.

### 5.3 Maps Required

| Map/view | Contents | Display and evidence rule |
|---|---|---|
| M06-A: Market-site condition | Verified footprint, baseline inventory, qualified visible-condition categories and unobservable areas. | Appropriate imagery/product required; no stall-level claim from medium-resolution imagery. |
| M06-B: Infrastructure presence/density | De-duplicated buildings and supporting assets in a declared area. | Presence separate from operation; units and denominator stated. |
| M09-B: Services and business context | Evidence-qualified water/WASH/power/storage/finance/logistics context. | Operating status/source date distinct from mapped feature. |
| M11-A: Neighborhood recovery signal, conditional | Quality-controlled matched-period radiance or other supported contextual series. | Neighborhood support and sensor/quality caveats; not turnover/GDP. |

The same map extent and profile logic should be repeated across sites where appropriate. Different resolution/evidence availability must remain visible rather than disguised by identical graphics.

### 5.4 Images Required

**IMG09: Matched physical-condition pair.** Licensed existing pre/post imagery of the same site, with suitable spatial support and registration. Each side needs date, provider, scale, and an explanation of what is interpretable.

**IMG10: Cleared site photographs.** Existing images tied to a specific observation period, showing the actual asset or service issue described. They must not be presented as photographs taken by this desk assessment.

**IMG11: Annotated site plan.** A derived cleared map identifying the existing market area, sub-sites, entrances, and proposed parcel only where verified. Proposed and existing assets use distinct symbology. No land-availability claim is made from visible open space.

An image swipe is optional, not mandatory. If one side is unsuitable or the change cannot be interpreted, use a single dated image with a qualified statement instead of a persuasive but misleading before/after comparison.

## Section 6: Vulnerability and Risk Analysis

### 6.1 Objectives

- Show recorded conflict exposure and documented disruption at a safe, appropriate scale.
- Examine flood hazard/event exposure, heat, water, and other relevant environmental constraints.
- Distinguish exposure screening from confirmed impact, quantified loss, or site-safety approval.
- Explain non-compensable implementation conditions before prioritization.

### 6.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Risks that shape recovery**

An intervention can address an immediate constraint and still remain vulnerable to disruption. Market buildings, access roads, supply links, utilities, and surrounding populations may be affected in different ways. Recovery planning must consider these dependencies alongside the visible condition of the site.

Recorded conflict events and documented interruptions provide evidence of exposure, with limits arising from reporting coverage, location precision, and timing. Their absence from a dataset does not establish that an area or journey is safe. Security/access requirements and explosive-remnant conditions remain separate questions for qualified, current review.

Flood analysis similarly distinguishes modelled river-hazard scenarios from dated observed inundation. Neither alone describes every local drainage problem or predicts a precise future impact at a stall or building. Heat, water, waste, and service conditions add further design and operating considerations, but remote observations cannot certify drinking-water quality or workplace safety.

The purpose of this screening is to identify pressures, uncertainty, and mitigation requirements. Some findings may support a more resilient design; others may indicate that enabling work or additional evidence is needed before an intervention can responsibly proceed.

**Transition:** These conditions must remain visible when markets are compared for rehabilitation preparation.

## Section 7: Market Prioritization

### 7.1 Objectives

- Explain the six-criterion screening model and its exact base weights.
- Separate need, opportunity/readiness, confidence, and investment eligibility.
- Show evidence coverage, score bounds, and sensitivity before presenting any rank.
- Keep high-need/low-readiness and insufficient-evidence markets visible in recovery planning.

### 7.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Comparing recovery opportunities**

Comparing markets requires a declared decision logic. A large potential catchment may indicate an opportunity to reach more residents, while poor services, disrupted connections, or unresolved implementation conditions may limit what rehabilitation can achieve. These considerations should be visible rather than compressed into an unexplained list of winners.

The screening model combines potential resident reach, accessibility, adapted functionality, lower recorded conflict exposure, economic-system significance, and rehabilitation feasibility. Its weights are fixed in the base comparison, and the definitions and scaling assumptions are documented. Higher scores represent stronger conditional opportunity/readiness on this model, not greater humanitarian need.

Missing evidence is not a low-performance observation. Where components are unknown, the model reports coverage and score bounds rather than silently replacing them with zero or reallocating their weights. Alternative population, network, and weighting scenarios show whether an apparent ordering is stable.

Investment preparation also requires an evidenced rehabilitation case and the relevant safeguards. A high score cannot resolve an unknown clearance, land, engineering, or maintenance condition. Markets with severe needs but blocked or uncertain readiness therefore remain in a separate portfolio of enabling actions and evidence requirements.

**Transition:** The purpose of comparison is to identify an appropriate next action, not merely produce a league table.

## Section 8: Rehabilitation Opportunities

### 8.1 Objectives

- Connect each candidate response to an evidenced constraint and plausible market-system benefit.
- Distinguish enabling measures, physical rehabilitation, and longer-term systems recovery.
- Include no-build/service alternatives, maintenance, inclusion, and implementation dependencies.
- Avoid unsupported cost, capacity, employment, income, or completion claims.

### 8.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: From constraints to rehabilitation**

A rehabilitation option should begin with the problem it is intended to solve. Damaged shelter, inadequate sanitation, a poorly connected trading location, an interrupted supply link, and lack of working capital are different constraints. They may require complementary measures, but they do not justify the same investment by default.

The assessment organizes options by their dependencies and preparation needs. Where permitted and safe, enabling measures may address documented access or basic-service constraints. More substantial rehabilitation requires qualified design, lawful permissions, environmental and social mitigation, and a credible operating and maintenance arrangement. Longer-term logistics, storage, energy, finance, or producer-market connections require their own feasibility and demand justification.

Inclusion is part of an effective trading environment. Accessible circulation, fair trading-space arrangements, safe facilities, and protection against displacement or exclusion should inform all options. These principles do not imply that affected traders' preferences or consent have been established by a desk assessment.

Each candidate package is therefore presented with its evidence, alternatives, conditions, responsible preparation roles, and unresolved questions. The objective is a useful next step toward recovery, not an unqualified promise that construction alone will restore business activity or livelihoods.

**Transition:** The final recommendations distinguish actions supported now from those that require further preparation or evidence.

## Section 9: Conclusions and Recommendations

### 9.1 Objectives

- Synthesize supported findings without introducing new unsupported claims.
- Distinguish immediate analytical/preparation actions from implementation decisions requiring gates.
- Identify owners, dependencies, refresh triggers, and unresolved evidence.
- Close the story with usable recommendations, methods/source access, and clear accountability for updates.

### 9.2 Narrative Text

**Display heading: Decisions and recommendations**

The central question is not only which market appears most damaged or most accessible, but which response can address an evidenced recovery need under workable and responsible conditions. A defensible recommendation connects the problem, the market's role, potential physical reach, service and livelihood constraints, and the requirements for implementation.

Secondary evidence can support that diagnosis and make assumptions more transparent. It can also reveal when a precise ranking, current operating-status statement, or site-level design recommendation would exceed what the sources can establish. Those limits should shape the next decision rather than disappear from the final map.

The recommended approach is to maintain distinct tracks for evidence-supported preparation, high-need locations requiring enabling conditions, and unresolved cases requiring better existing information. Any move toward works must retain the relevant security, explosive-remnant, land, engineering, environmental/social, and operating requirements.

The assessment should remain a dated decision-support resource. Material changes in access, population presence, services, conflict, flooding, or source quality may change the conclusion. Clear ownership, a documented refresh process, and a traceable record of revisions are therefore part of the recovery approach, not an afterthought to publication.

**Closing sentence:** The value of the assessment lies in making the next recovery decision more specific, more accountable, and better grounded in the evidence available.
