# Maps, Dashboards, Infographics and Visualisation Specifications

## 1. Product Scope and Status

This document defines Deliverable F: the complete visual-product production catalogue for the Khartoum Market Assessment StoryMap. It specifies what each product should communicate, the data and analytical outputs it requires, how it should be designed and symbolized, and what interpretations it may support.

The detailed specification for every product is in [Deliverable_F_Visual_Product_Register.csv](Deliverable_F_Visual_Product_Register.csv). Each record contains **title, purpose, datasets, GIS methodology, visual design, symbology, and key insights generated**, together with dependencies, StoryMap sections, aliases/views, and release conditions. Dataset identifiers resolve to the names, sources, URLs, coverage, resolution, limitations and acquisition status in [Deliverable_D_Data_Inventory.csv](Deliverable_D_Data_Inventory.csv).

The register contains **67 specification records**:

| Product type | Records | Production interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Map families | 13 | M01-M13, with 31 named narrative views retained from Deliverable C. |
| Dashboards | 5 | D01-D05; may be coordinated views in one application rather than five independent apps. |
| Infographics/scorecards | 4 | IG01-IG04; qualified aliases for the original infographic I01-I04 IDs. |
| StoryMap charts and diagrams | 27 | CH01-CH27, retaining the storyboard identifiers. |
| Additional analytical views | 2 | VC01-VC02, supporting the evidence-coverage and sensitivity views specified in Deliverable E. |
| Media briefs | 16 | IMG01-IMG16, including existing images, annotated plans, and conditional comparison panels. |

These are not 67 independent GIS analyses, acquired assets, required simultaneous embeds, or claims of completed output. Several products reuse the same reviewed analytical result. The ten EV01-EV10 prioritisation views are mapped to these products below rather than duplicated as ten competing score calculations.

All insights are prospective: **what a properly populated and validated product could establish**. No actual current market location, damage percentage, reach total, conflict exposure, flood impact, ranking or investment benefit is generated by this catalogue. Existing source data remain subject to acquisition, suitability, rights and review requirements.

`NONE_METHOD` in the dataset field means a methodological illustration has no observational dataset. Its actual dependency is the approved configuration or methodology, not an invented empirical source. Dependencies such as `OD_TOTAL`, `MCDA_OUTPUT` and `GATE_REGISTER` are proposed derived tables/layers defined by the GIS and prioritisation workflows; they are not files already supplied.

## 2. Shared Cartographic and Publication Standards

### 2.1 Required Analytical Metadata

Every analytical map, chart, dashboard selection and scorecard must identify:

- The actual observation period or image-acquisition date, separately from publication/retrieval date.
- Geographic support: market footprint, neighborhood, origin grid, locality, corridor, or national context.
- Variable, unit, numerator/denominator and valid coverage where relevant.
- Population/network/mode/market-set/hazard scenario and model version where applicable.
- Source and derived-run references, evidence class and principal limitations.
- Unknown, unobservable, unallocated, stale, conflicting or unsuitable evidence as appropriate.

Use the adopted administrative boundary/version and verified name crosswalk. Coordinates for unresolved markets remain blank; an unresolved directory entry must not be assigned to a neighborhood centroid merely to appear on a map.

### 2.2 Visual Language

Use an organizationally approved StoryMaps/ArcGIS theme. A restrained illustrative palette is water/locational blue `#0072B2`, supported operation teal `#007F75`, constraints amber `#9C5B00`, recorded exposure rust `#A63434`, and neutral unknown fill `#E7E7E7` with dark text/hatching. These are suggestions, not official UNDP brand specifications or proof of contrast compliance.

Use sequential color for nonnegative ordered quantities and a diverging scale with a meaningful zero for signed change/anomaly. Use categories, shapes and text for qualitative roles, evidence/gate states and missingness. Test contrast in the actual background/component combination; color must never be the sole carrier of meaning.

Avoid decorative 3D population columns, pictorial trader/job counts, animated flows with no measured quantities, complete radar charts with missing axes filled at zero, and green "safe" areas created from zero reported events. Quantitative symbol area must follow the legend's variable; do not make symbol radius proportional to population while labelling area as proportional.

### 2.3 Scale, Comparison and Classification

Use local metric analysis in an appropriate projection, normally EPSG:32636 after extent review; display in the platform's supported CRS without recalculating areas from Web Mercator. Long national routes require appropriate geodesic/regional treatment. Static maps need a suitable scale bar and orientation where useful, a boundary disclaimer and readable source/date notes.

Keep classification breaks, units, extent and rendering comparable across scenario/time panels. If breaks change, say so and avoid implying direct visual comparability. Use data-specific scientifically meaningful boundaries or explicitly approved display intervals rather than automatic quantiles that change with every selection. Retain raw values in accessible tables/popups.

A 90 m hazard grid or coarse climate product must not appear to deliver independent stall-level values after resampling. Clearly distinguish native support from reporting grid and display resolution. Raster masks/nodata need separate legend treatment; transparent nodata that resembles an unexposed background is unacceptable.

### 2.4 Delivery and Accessibility

Each essential analytical product needs a legible static/text alternative. Interactive output may be a web map, Dashboard or Experience Builder view, but readers should not require an undocumented interaction to understand the conclusion. Provide descriptive titles, accessible tables or text summaries, meaningful alt text, keyboard-compatible controls, and desktop/mobile layouts with no clipped legends or overlaid text.

Source dates and analytical caveats are substantive content. Reader-facing narrative should not describe clicks, scrolling, shortcuts or how the interface works. Use familiar controls, concise labels and tooltips in the application instead.

A picture, swipe, app or map is released only after source/rights/classification review. Public service records and fields must be sanitized; hiding a popup field is not access control. Existing material marked `NOT YET CLASSIFIED` cannot be reused externally without the appropriate owner clearance.

## 3. Map Specifications

### M01. Market Locations and Hierarchy

**Purpose:** Establish the actual study geography, selected cohort and documented market roles without implying a complete census or priority order.

**Datasets:** DS001/DS003 adopted geography; DS004 existing market identity records; DS005 OSM corroboration; DS047 hydrographic context; DS083 original scope. Use only verified adopted geometries and role evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Resolve aliases and duplicates, distinguish markets from sub-sites, validate point/footprint placement, spatially join adopted localities and classify documented hierarchy/functions. Maintain a nonspatial unresolved/excluded list and comparison-universe version.

**Visual design:** Move from Sudan locator to state/locality and then role views. Keep labels restrained and use bilingual approved names. A locator marker is not a verified entrance.

**Symbology:** Distinct shapes for retail/mixed/wholesale/specialized roles; clear water and thin locality boundaries; no priority ramp. Unresolved sites are not mapped at approximate coordinates without an expressly different contextual use.

**Key insights generated:** Could establish where confirmed sites sit relative to localities and major physical features, which roles are documented, and where cohort coverage or identity remains incomplete. It cannot establish current operation or state-wide representativeness.

### M02. Supply Chains and Trade Routes

**Purpose:** Explain reported origin-hub-market connections and distinguish them from modelled transport alternatives and conditional producer-market links.

**Datasets:** DS004, DS021-DS023, DS025, DS033, DS067-DS069 and DS085 where relevant. DS068 is usable only after the actual SAMRA report is received and reviewed.

**GIS methodology:** Extract commodity, season, period, origin, hub, destination, source and quantity only when reported. Geocode supported nodes. Use schematic links if a precise route is not documented; route modelled alternatives on the reviewed graph and preserve actual route geometry and scenario.

**Visual design:** Regional-to-local map sequence with one commodity/season emphasis at a time. Avoid a cluttered all-commodity network. Equal line widths when no compatible measured volume exists.

**Symbology:** Solid reported connections, dashed modelled alternatives, direction arrows and consistent node roles. Route status/date is explicit; no animated throughput speed or Sankey width without data.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify documented supplier connections, shared hubs, missing links and candidate bottlenecks requiring complementary action. It cannot measure tonnage, supplier shares or current safe passage from network geometry.

### M03. Population Distribution and Potential Catchments

**Purpose:** Present the population basis, compatible movement context and conditional potential resident reach.

**Datasets:** DS001, one selected DS007/DS009/DS015 baseline, compatible DS008/DS010/DS012-DS014 information, and reviewed transport DS021. Alternatives are sensitivity inputs, not additive populations.

**GIS methodology:** Audit counts versus density, conserve native totals through transformations, reconcile stocks/flows/periods, construct documented scenarios and join total-cost OD reach to origins. Separate individual overlapping catchments, union reach and unique allocation.

**Visual design:** Separate baseline-density, movement and reach panels. Identify which population is historical, estimated, projected or hypothetical. Do not mix national demographic estimates into fine current occupancy without a model.

**Symbology:** Sequential density with actual units; categorical allocated/unallocated/unknown states; scenario intervals in linked summaries. Cumulative returns are not stacked onto an already inclusive total.

**Key insights generated:** Could reveal potential resident concentration, access gaps and sensitivity to population assumptions. It cannot establish actual customers, footfall, unique beneficiaries or a contemporary census.

### M04. Accessibility and Transport Connectivity

**Purpose:** Describe conditional walking/freight burden, disconnection and dependence on roads/crossings.

**Datasets:** DS004 verified entrances; DS007/DS010 population context; DS021/DS022 graph and restrictions; DS023/DS024 dated corridor evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Build true same-level connectivity and mode restrictions, verify first/last-mile connectors, route toward entrances, charge connector costs once, and preserve unlocated/disconnected/beyond-threshold/failed states. OD-A supplies threshold reach; unrestricted OD-B supplies local reachability/burden. Recompute coherent disruption scenarios.

**Visual design:** Walking 15/30/60-minute views separated from freight and reference/disruption comparisons. Use route geometry for corridor analysis; OD straight display lines are not paths travelled.

**Symbology:** Ordered travel-time bands; disconnected and data-unresolved categories visibly distinct. Restricted/unknown assumptions require labels rather than an implied safe-route legend.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify potential reach deficits, high burden, crossing dependence and lost connectivity under a named scenario. It does not measure observed time, fares, safety, universal disability access or consumer preference.

### M05. Modelled Market Influence Zones

**Purpose:** Show unique nearest-network-time allocation among an explicit eligible set, not behavioral market shares.

**Datasets:** DS004, one selected population baseline plus compatible DS010/DS012/DS013 context, and DS021/DS022; derived valid OD costs and eligible market-set version.

**GIS methodology:** Collapse entrances to minimum valid market time, assign each origin once to a nearest eligible market within the declared threshold, break exact ties deterministically, and preserve unallocated/data-unresolved population. Dissolve origin reporting cells only for display.

**Visual design:** Discrete allocated zones with candidate-set, population and network scenario stated. Complete origin accounting accompanies any aggregate.

**Symbology:** Categorical color by assigned market, clear unallocated/unknown patterns, and verified market points. Do not use a geometric Thiessen result labelled as network influence across rivers.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify how candidate eligibility and network conditions redistribute potential resident allocation and leave gaps. It does not show exclusive trading territory, measured preference, customer loyalty or calibrated competitive capture.

### M06. Infrastructure Density and Visible Physical Condition

**Purpose:** Separate mapped asset presence/density from qualified visible condition and its observation coverage.

**Datasets:** DS004, one deduplicated adopted DS026/DS027/DS028 inventory, relevant DS029/DS031 facilities, DS035 records, DS041 and/or suitable licensed DS042 imagery. DS036 provides area context, not a substitute for building-scale interpretation.

**GIS methodology:** Match baseline vintage, register suitable imagery, classify observable structures and qualifying visible condition, and report damaged/observable plus observable/baseline fractions. Calculate footprint m2/ha or unique assets per declared area without source overlap duplication.

**Visual design:** Separate site-condition and neighborhood-density panels. Maintain consistent extents/scales for comparisons without disguising different evidence resolution.

**Symbology:** Condition categories with explicit possible-change/unobservable patterns; restrained sequential density; no no-visible-damage-equals-safe designation. Standardize the visible-condition legend across reviewers/products.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify visible asset deficits, density relationships and interpretation gaps. It cannot establish structural integrity, interior damage, current trader counts, utility operation or ownership.

### M07. Recorded Conflict Exposure and Documented Disruption

**Purpose:** Present permitted recorded exposure and transport/market interruption evidence at a support appropriate to source precision.

**Datasets:** Authorized DS016 or an explicitly alternative DS017; scoped DS018/DS020/DS024/DS079 documents. Shared events/reposts are not independent counts.

**GIS methodology:** Filter relevant violence categories, deduplicate provider/revision IDs, review location/date precision, apply stated windows/buffers and summarize permitted areas. Analyse documented corridors separately using actual paths. Retain excluded/coarse/possible records and reporting caveats.

**Visual design:** Generalized public summaries, with restricted detail only in an authorized environment. Show observation window and reporting support, not a live operational picture.

**Symbology:** Muted sequential recorded quantities, dated disruption symbols and separate unknown coverage. Zero qualifying records does not become a green safe zone; an event density is not attack probability.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify recorded temporal/spatial patterns and documented interruptions relevant to recovery dependencies. It cannot certify absence of violence, safe routes, explosive clearance or protection conditions.

### M08. River Flood Hazard and Observed Exposure

**Purpose:** Distinguish modelled river-hazard scenarios, their exposed assets/population, and separately observed dated flood events.

**Datasets:** DS004 verified footprints; selected population and transport/building assets DS007/DS021/DS026; DS043 raw depth and masks; applicable DS044 event extents; relevant DS045/DS047/DS053 context.

**GIS methodology:** Use the correct global 3 arc-second/about 90 m product and raw depth for arbitrary thresholds. Apply permanent-water/spurious-depth/nodata controls; calculate native-support fractional asset, length and population exposures with coverage/unknowns. Keep event observations separate from return periods and avoid small-zone raster omission.

**Visual design:** Same-extent hazard/event panels with distinct labels, threshold, return period or actual event date. No single undifferentiated flood-risk surface created by adding sources.

**Symbology:** Sequential depth with units; permanent water distinct; observed flood overlay separate; uncertain/uncovered areas hatched. Return-period outputs are alternatives, not additive exposures.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify asset/access exposure and areas needing more specific drainage/mitigation evidence. It does not quantify monetary losses, prove damage, certify plot suitability or capture all pluvial/urban flood processes.

### M09. Supporting Services and Business Ecosystem

**Purpose:** Show supporting institutions and service relationships while preserving mapped presence versus operation.

**Datasets:** DS029-DS034, relevant DS058/DS060/DS061, and permitted DS092 records. Healthsites/OSM overlap and facility point/area duplication require review.

**GIS methodology:** Resolve facility identities, classify services and source dates, separately join applicable operating evidence, and calculate declared Euclidean/network proximity only as physical context. Preserve capacity units and unknown service state.

**Visual design:** Theme-by-theme facility/service views rather than every icon on one map. Use compact status/source popups and de-emphasize unrelated facilities.

**Symbology:** Familiar health/education/water/storage/finance/transport symbols with an explicit mapped-only outline and text-qualified status. Do not let an operating marker imply reliable, affordable or equitable access.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify service dependencies, nearby mapped support and gaps in current operation evidence. It cannot establish liquidity, credit approval, institutional buying, business sales or full service capacity from proximity.

### M10. Environmental Conditions Affecting Market Recovery

**Purpose:** Provide decision-relevant environmental context without falsely merging unlike scales and variables.

**Datasets:** DS036/DS039 vegetation, DS040 surface heat, DS045 terrain, DS048 upstream basins, DS049 or DS050 coherent rainfall series, DS051 regional atmospheric context, DS052 land cover, DS053 surface water and DS069 supplier-area evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Apply product-specific masks/physical scaling, compare seasons, calculate NDVI and rainfall anomalies only at appropriate support, and distinguish surface/air temperature and surface water/utility service. Do not splice CHIRPS versions or interpret WorldCover algorithm changes as pure actual change.

**Visual design:** Small multiples or supported map states for heat, rainfall/upstream context, land cover, vegetation and water; no all-risk index with hidden weights.

**Symbology:** Sequential temperature, zero-centered anomaly colors, source-consistent categorical land classes and water context. Each legend has its own unit and observation support; invalid/unknown remains distinct.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify environmental design pressures and producer-area context requiring complementary analysis. It cannot establish crop yield, market income, safe drinking water, groundwater yield, occupational heat stress or engineered drainage performance.

### M11. Neighborhood Economic Recovery Signals

**Purpose:** Show qualified radiance and documentary signals as contextual evidence, not market output.

**Datasets:** DS055 or a separately selected DS056 collection, optional DS054 fire context, and scoped DS018/DS034/DS079 status/service evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Select consistent platform/collection and matched windows, apply product QA and valid-observation/area checks, retain gap-fill flags, use a positive above-noise baseline for percentage change, and review fire, grid restoration and residential spillover.

**Visual design:** Neighborhood-level change paired with dated status/service evidence and a clear radiance trend. A small market occupies less than an independently interpretable light footprint.

**Symbology:** Signed radiance-change colors around zero; masks for insufficient observations/noise; absolute change where percent is invalid. No labels such as GDP recovered or jobs restored.

**Key insights generated:** Could show broad disruption/restoration patterns and their concurrence with existing reports. It does not measure turnover, employment, causal intervention impact or isolated market recovery.

### M12. Conditional Rehabilitation Opportunity and Preparation Status

**Purpose:** Communicate eligible analytical comparisons while keeping need, confidence, missingness and investment gates separate.

**Datasets:** Eligible outputs from the six-criterion workflow supported by DS004/DS006/DS007/DS016/DS019/DS021/DS035/DS066/DS074/DS075 and their actual acquired equivalents; approved base configuration and gate/need records.

**GIS methodology:** Join one market-period-scenario-model result, validate all leaf coverage and uncertainty, retain complete/provisional/insufficient status and independent gate/additionality conditions, then compare only approved coherent scenarios. No synthetic examples enter real layers.

**Visual design:** Separate opportunity, need/preparation, scenario-stability and concluding-decision views. Use a readable table/popups for detail rather than encode every property in one complex symbol.

**Symbology:** Approved ordered score classes for complete comparable cases; provisional/unknown hatching; explicit gate and need text. Display intervals must not be described as thresholds of approval. No missing value is plotted as the lowest performance class.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify supported preparation candidates, unstable orderings and high-need blocked/evidence-gap portfolios. It cannot rank humanitarian worth, authorize works or allocate funds automatically.

### M13. Candidate Rehabilitation Packages and Dependencies

**Purpose:** Locate conditional responses, alternatives and prerequisites, not an approved construction portfolio.

**Datasets:** Existing site/permission and works records DS004/DS006/DS019/DS032/DS034/DS035/DS074/DS075, documented connections DS067, inclusion DS077 and cost evidence DS091 only where actually received.

**GIS methodology:** Link a documented constraint to the existing market/sub-site/proposed parcel, service/access dependencies, options including no-build, maintenance arrangements and scenario robustness. Distinguish proposed, feasible, approved and implemented states.

**Visual design:** Package-level overview and selected site dependency plans with concise condition panels. State why an option addresses a constraint rather than simply show icons for planned assets.

**Symbology:** Solid existing outlines, dashed proposed elements and categorical package symbols; unresolved prerequisite labels. Parcel detail is released only when lawful and permitted.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify the appropriate next preparation step and complementary conditions. It does not establish land availability, engineering design, tender costs, consent, construction approval or achieved benefits.

## 4. Dashboard Specifications

### D01. Market Profile and Evidence Dashboard

**Purpose:** Provide the full directory and a comparable evidence-qualified profile for each resolved or nonspatially listed market.

**Datasets:** DS004, applicable DS032/DS034/DS035/DS041/DS042/DS061/DS063/DS066/DS092 records, and the validated profile/observation tables. The CSV lists the complete reference set; entries are requirements/candidates, not acquired evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Build one market-period-scenario-model summary grain and separate linked long-form observations. Count unique markets, not shelters/entrances/claims. A selected site may show operation, availability/completeness, physical observation coverage, services, finance evidence, access context and missing reasons.

**Visual design:** Map/directory beside a compact profile, service/item matrices and evidence list. In a coordinated application, route to other analytical views using the same stable market ID and tested state transfer.

**Symbology:** Reuse M01/M06/M09 legends. Unknown labels and dates remain visible; no missing-zero circular gauges or implied universal market-health percentage.

**Key insights generated:** Could establish what is supported at a particular site, what constraint is documented, and what evidence remains missing. It cannot produce unobserved traders, turnover, participation or food-safety findings.

**Controls and aggregation:** Market/locality/type/period selectors, coherent scenario state, source/evidence access and a nonspatial unresolved directory. Market selection filters all relevant widgets by declared keys. Public evidence links must not expose controlled raw records.

### D02. Potential Resident Reach Dashboard

**Purpose:** Compare potential population reach and uncertainty without duplicate residents or implied customer counts.

**Datasets:** One chosen DS007/DS009/DS015 baseline, compatible DS010/DS012/DS013 inputs, DS021/DS022 and the conserved origin/scenario/allocation outputs.

**GIS methodology:** Aggregate unique allocation or origin-union reach under one eligible market set. Display individual overlapping catchments only as explicitly non-additive context. Retain assigned, beyond-threshold, disconnected, no-eligible-market and data-unresolved categories.

**Visual design:** Selected-scenario potential reach, a range/comparison panel, map and mutually exclusive access/assignment distribution. Population product/period and modelled-current versus baseline status are prominent.

**Symbology:** Ordered time bands, categorical allocation and distinct unknown states. Low/central/high bars are alternatives, never stacked as one total.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify potential reach, origin gaps, overlap and sensitivity to resident/network assumptions. It does not establish observed shopping, footfall, safe return or unique assistance beneficiaries.

**Controls and aggregation:** One scenario/period/mode at a time, named eligible market set, threshold and selected geography. Any headline total recomputes from unique origins, not summed market service areas or duplicated observation joins.

### D03. Accessibility and Network Resilience Dashboard

**Purpose:** Compare burden, disconnection and scenario dependence for walking and freight.

**Datasets:** DS004/DS007 and reviewed DS021-DS024 with actual connector, total OD and scenario outputs.

**GIS methodology:** Use unrestricted OD-B for local reachability and weighted medians, total-cost OD-A for threshold catchments, and separate freight route results. Recompute statistic weights from origins rather than average market medians. Keep computational/location failures distinct from physical disconnection.

**Visual design:** Mode/network/speed selector; burden distribution; paired scenario changes; affected market list; optional route detail where cleared. No state median manufactured from a mean of medians.

**Symbology:** Units in minutes; ordered time classes; explicit disconnected/unresolved statuses; categorical loss of route rather than a finite detour for unreachable destinations.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify where modeled access is burdensome or depends on a shared crossing. It does not establish actual journey time, fares, security or equal accessibility for all groups.

**Controls and aggregation:** Scenarios must change all dependent widgets. Freight and pedestrian results use separate denominators/modes; a chart cannot compare them on a common unlabeled scale.

### D04. Recorded Conflict and Disruption Dashboard

**Purpose:** Review recorded events, precision limitations and documentary interruptions safely.

**Datasets:** Authorized DS016 or alternative DS017 and applicable DS018/DS024/DS079 claims; derived event-precision audit and permitted summaries.

**GIS methodology:** Count distinct eligible event IDs within the selected geographic union/window. Deduplicate original lineage and retain precision exclusions/date uncertainty. Separate event counts from recency weights and documentary interruption records.

**Visual design:** Generalized map, window trend, precision/exclusion summary and scoped source list. Internal authorized event detail is not automatically suitable for a public embedded dashboard.

**Symbology:** Recorded quantities in a restrained sequential ramp, explicit no-qualifying-records/unknown distinction and text for report-only interruptions. No green safe condition, live checkpoint view or event count presented as probability.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify recorded concentrations, timing and weaknesses in the evidence. It cannot establish true violence prevalence, absence of coercion, safe routes or clearance.

**Controls and aggregation:** Approved category/window/geography filters with defaults documented; no total formed by adding overlapping market buffers. Public fields, tooltip contents, downloads and service responses require explicit permission review.

### D05. Economic Recovery and Market Conditions Dashboard

**Purpose:** Compare applicable price, operation, service and neighborhood radiance evidence without collapsing them into an unsupported economic-output index.

**Datasets:** Applicable DS032/DS034/DS055/DS056/DS061/DS063-DS066/DS093 records and validated price, status and radiance tables.

**GIS methodology:** Harmonize item/grade/unit/market/retail-wholesale/currency/period; preserve nominal/real/rate-regime distinctions and missing observation periods. Independently validate radiance products, quality and neighborhood support. Bracket recovery only when repeated evidence supports it.

**Visual design:** Aligned small multiples, a clear observed-status sequence, series completeness and source/period notes. Do not place unlike indicators on a common misleading y-axis or summarize them as income recovered.

**Symbology:** Consistent styles per comparable series; gaps remain broken; projected/modelled periods visually distinct; radiance and price scales separate. Unknown operating evidence is text, not zero recovery.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify concurrence or divergence between documented services, prices and contextual lighting. It cannot establish causation, profitability, market GDP or livelihood gains.

**Controls and aggregation:** Site/neighborhood, period, comparable item and indicator. Do not imply market-level light resolution from a site selector. No mixed-unit price averages or extrapolation across unobserved dates.

## 5. Infographics and Scorecards

### IG01. Market Recovery Scorecard

**Purpose:** Provide one portable, evidence-qualified market decision summary. This is the original infographic I01, renamed with an IG prefix only to distinguish it from analytical indicator I01.

**Datasets:** Eligible profile/MCDA/gate outputs supported by DS004/DS006/DS019/DS032/DS035/DS066/DS074/DS075 and actual linked evidence.

**GIS methodology:** Select one market-period-scenario-model; assemble raw values, normalized components or bounds, evidence coverage/confidence, need, implementation conditions and up to three conditional next actions. Do not calculate a second score for the graphic.

**Visual design:** One-page printable profile with locator, compact component bars/intervals and readable evidence/action panels. Exact dates and missingness remain legible at the intended print/display size.

**Symbology:** Supported values versus unknown/interval labels; consistent criterion colors; explicit gate text. No filled complete radar polygon or decorative jobs icons.

**Key insights generated:** Could explain why a particular market has a documented recovery constraint and what decision remains conditional. It does not measure humanitarian worth or actual percent recovery.

### IG02. Market Comparison and Ranking Matrix

**Purpose:** Explain approved trade-offs and eligible comparison without hiding gaps, need or gates. This is the original infographic I02 and shares its analytical asset with CH20/EV02.

**Datasets:** Approved component/score/bound/gate/need outputs, based on the actual source chain including DS004/DS006/DS007/DS016/DS019/DS021/DS035/DS066/DS074/DS075.

**GIS methodology:** Use twenty-leaf missingness accounting and six-criterion contributions. Separate complete analytical ranks from provisional bounds and investment eligibility; preserve method/scenario/candidate-set versions.

**Visual design:** Aligned criterion columns with contributions, score/bounds, coverage, scenario rank range, need and gate/action state. Incomplete cases have a separate unranked presentation, not bottom-ranked zeros.

**Symbology:** Stable criterion colors accompanied by numbers, missing-cell hatching and interval marks. Score display classes are descriptive, not funding approval categories.

**Key insights generated:** Could show why comparable sites differ and whether an ordering is defensible. It cannot decide awards automatically or convert MCDA points into benefit or success probability.

### IG03. Livelihood Constraint and Opportunity Matrix

## 7. Additional Analytical Views and Prioritisation Crosswalk

### VC01. Evidence Completeness and Within-Criterion Coverage

**Purpose:** Explain why a point comparison is complete, provisional or insufficient and which documentation is missing.

**Datasets:** No direct external observation family is sufficient on its own. Use the actual observation/eligibility table, component definitions and approved base configuration; trace each leaf back to its acquired source in Deliverable D.

**GIS methodology:** Sum eligible effective leaf weights overall and within each criterion, preserving failed eligibility and unknown reasons. Do not count nonblank columns as weighted coverage or turn source confidence into a score discount. Show the separate minimum coverage rules and complete-point requirement.

**Visual design:** A total covered-weight strip with six criterion rows and a missing-reason table; the selected market/period/scenario/version remains visible.

**Symbology:** Covered versus unknown patterns and percentages; neutral documentary styling, not red/green market performance. Confidence is a separate field and legend.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify the evidence that prevents comparison and whether an uncertainty interval reflects a small leaf gap or a whole missing criterion. It cannot establish low substantive performance from low documentation.

### VC02. Sensitivity and Assumption-Influence View

**Purpose:** Identify which approved assumptions/policy choices materially change an eligible comparison.

**Datasets:** Approved configuration and recomputed scenarios based on actual affected DS007/DS016/DS021/DS022/DS043 or other qualified inputs. Synthetic examples are excluded from real output.

**GIS methodology:** Recompute relative weight perturbations, valid anchors, population/network cases and other approved structural tests. Preserve dependencies and the comparison universe; separate scenario absence/ineligibility from bottom rank. Use coherent reruns, not arbitrary score subtractions.

**Visual design:** Tornado chart of score differences for one market or a scenario-by-market stability matrix; baseline, units, eligible denominator and changed candidate set stated.

**Symbology:** Signed change around zero, explicit ineligible markers, and labelled approved scenario types. Do not interpret sensitivity frequency as a success probability.

**Key insights generated:** Could identify model/policy-dependent orderings and unstable shortlist membership. It does not calibrate forecasts or repair unsuitable evidence.

10. Release approved instances with an owner, refresh triggers and an archived dependency/run manifest.

An apparent score improvement caused by improved documentation, revised anchors or a changed candidate set is not automatically real-world recovery. Explain data/method changes in a version log. Never replace a failed/unknown gate with an older favorable record merely to preserve a published ranking.
